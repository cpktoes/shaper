<objective>
Give the shaper one more setting that follows them around, exactly the way the Imperial/Metric
chooser already does: a tick-box on the rails screen that says whether the rail band instructions
sheet should be included when they print. It starts unticked, it is remembered on their account and
in their browser, and it never becomes part of a saved board.

Purpose: PRNT-05 needs somewhere durable to store "how this shaper prints". D-06 settles that this
describes the shaper, not the board — so it sits beside units, not inside the design snapshot.

Output: a second per-shaper preference, built on the units machinery rather than beside it, plus the
rails sidebar control that sets it.
</objective>

<assumption_delta_decision>
**Now-primary noun:** a per-shaper *preferences record* — the `user_preferences` row keyed by
`clerk_user_id`. Until this phase the app had exactly one shaper preference (units); from this phase
it has two, and CONTEXT.md's "Provider shape" discretion left open whether that plural should be
modelled as one provider carrying a record or two providers side by side.

**Decision: `add-alongside`.** `useUnits()` is called from 24 files across every design screen, the
settings menu, the order form and the two template builders. Merging both preferences into one
provider would put a shipped, trusted feature with a zero-regression bar inside the blast radius of
all 24 for no behavioural gain. So `UnitsProvider` / `useUnits()` keep their exact current shape and
a sibling `PrintInstructionsProvider` / `usePrintRailInstructions()` mounts beside them — while the
*pure* logic that actually encodes the rules (the five-branch handoff and the bounded write queue) is
generalized once into `lib/preference-handoff.ts` and called by both. That satisfies D-07's "extend
rather than duplicate" where duplication would actually hurt — in the rules — without touching a
single `useUnits()` call site. This matches RESEARCH.md's own recommendation and its assumption A1,
now confirmed against the real call-site count.

**What would force a later promote to one preferences provider:** a third preference, or the first
preference whose resolved value depends on another's (at which point the two providers'
reconciliation order becomes observable and must be sequenced in one place). Either of those, and
`lib/preference-handoff.ts` already holds the rules — only the React wrapper would need merging.

**Invariant test (suggested, not required):** `lib/preference-handoff.test.ts` encodes the
generalized intent directly — for any preference value type, the account wins over the browser on
sign-in, an explicit browser pick promotes only into an empty account, and a default nobody chose is
never written to either side.
</assumption_delta_decision>
