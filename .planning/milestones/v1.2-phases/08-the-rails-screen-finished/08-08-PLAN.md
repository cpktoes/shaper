<objective>
With Metric chosen, the little example rail on the INSTRUCTIONS tab is unreadable along its edges:
the numbers under the bottom axis run into one unbroken string of digits, and the numbers down the
left side are cut off by the edge of the drawing, so 80 reads as 30 and "10 mm" reads as "m". The
same drawing is fine in Imperial, and fine in Metric when it is drawn larger — the numbers are
pinned to a screen size, so they stay the same size while the drawing shrinks around them.

Purpose: RAIL-06. A shaper reading the teaching card in millimetres deserves the same clear numbers
the Imperial reader already gets.

Output: the axis numbers thin out and move when there is no room for them, and stay exactly where
they are when there is.
</objective>

## What is already built (do not rebuild)

`RailSectionPlot`, `buildRailPlotGrid`'s 10 mm Metric pitch, the callout system and its de-overlap
pass all shipped in 08-01 and the two quick tasks after it, and all of them are correct. The Metric
tick maths is right — the ticks are on the correct millimetre positions and read through the display
boundary. Only the *labels* need to know how much room they have. Change nothing about the grid
positions, the plot's box, or the mark-name callouts.

    `buildRailPlotGrid` an optional third argument. Its first field decides how many of the 10 mm

    5 every 50 mm. Ticks that lose their number come back with an empty label — the grid line and the
    little tick mark stay on every 10 mm step, because the grid is what a shaper counts squares on;
    only the numbers thin. The millimetre mark moves with them, onto the first *labelled* non-zero
    tick outward from the origin, so the axis still says what unit it is in exactly once. The
    Imperial branch ignores the argument entirely — do not edit it.

    their spacing in inches times `SCALE` times the render scale; each label's width on screen is its
    character count times the moved `CALLOUT_CHAR_PX` (the face is pinned in screen pixels, so a
    label's screen width does not change with the render scale — only the gap does). Two neighbours
    fit when the gap is at least the mean of their two widths plus a small margin. Use a 2 px margin
    and say in the comment that it is the same slack, for the same reason, that
    `RAIL_CALLOUT_OVERLAP_MARGIN` folds into the mark-name overlap test: the character width is an
    estimate, not a measurement. This is the pair-wise text-extent idiom the mark names already use —
    not a bucket or a threshold on the render scale.

    **(d) Wire it up, Metric only.** In `RailSectionPlot`, when the system is Metric, try 1, then 2,

    largest if none do). Build the final grid with that choice, and skip drawing a text element for a
    tick whose label is empty. When the system is Imperial, do not consult the fit test at all — an
    Imperial plot must be unable to change.

    At the three measured scales this gives: the INSTRUCTIONS card thins to a number every 20 mm; the

    The left-hand numbers are drawn in the 22-unit strip reserved for them and clipped by the
    drawing's own edge when they outgrow it. In Metric they outgrow it two ways: "10 mm" is five
    characters wide, and at the card's size even a two-digit number is wider than the strip. Fix it
    in two steps, both Metric-only, both purely about where text is drawn — no padding, no bounds and
    no drawn coordinate may change, because the View Full Sized dialog derives the rail's true
    physical size from those bounds.

    **Step 1 — carry the unit only where it fits.** Add a second field to `buildRailPlotGrid`'s
    options that turns the millimetre mark on the *left* axis off, defaulting to on so today's
    behaviour is the default. In `RailSectionPlot`, when the system is Metric, ask a second exported
    fit test whether the widest left-hand label fits the strip: its width on screen (character count
    times `CALLOUT_CHAR_PX`) against the strip's width on screen (the pad less the label's own gap,
    times the render scale). If the suffixed labels do not fit, rebuild without the millimetre mark.
    The plot still names its unit once, on the bottom axis, which is what CLAUDE.md's "carried once"
    rule asks for. At all three measured scales this drops the left-hand millimetre mark — which is
    exactly the label the UAT saw clipped in the VIEWER and poking off the printed sheet.

    **Step 2 — when even the bare numbers do not fit, draw them inside.** If the widest bare
    left-hand label still does not fit the strip (this happens only on the INSTRUCTIONS card, at
    0.785), draw the left-hand numbers just *inside* the plot instead — anchored to start from a
    little right of the axis line, clear of the tick mark's inboard arm — wearing the same
    `textShadow` halo the mark names already use so they read cleanly over the faint grid. The tick
    marks themselves do not move. Nothing is ever clipped again, at any size, because a label that
    cannot fit outside is drawn inside rather than off the edge.

    Keep both steps Imperial-blind: an Imperial label is a single digit that has always fitted, and
    an Imperial plot must be unable to change.

    **Tests** in `components/rails/rail-section-plot.test.ts`:
    - turning the left-axis unit mark off leaves every left-hand label a bare number and does not
      touch the bottom axis's own mark;
    - Imperial output is identical with and without the new option;
    - the left-hand fit test, at the three measured scales, says: the suffixed labels fit at none of
      them (0.785, 1.2, 2.3); the bare labels fit at 1.2 and 2.3 but not at 0.785 — so the VIEWER and
