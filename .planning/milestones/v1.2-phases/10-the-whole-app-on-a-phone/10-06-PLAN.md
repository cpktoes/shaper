<objective>
**Make a board card fit the screen it is actually on.**

The shaper's verdict on a real phone was blunt: *"Two is fine but they are way too tall. Even
vertical, the boards are bigger than the screen."* The card was built to a fixed height — 550 dots,
chosen from screenshots of one emulated phone — and a fixed height is right for exactly one screen
and wrong for every other. Turned sideways it was worse than wrong: the card grew to 757 dots on a
screen with 237 dots of room, because the cap that held it down was switched off by the same
width rule plan 10-05 has just fixed.

So the cap stops being a number of dots and becomes a share of the screen: **about three-quarters of
what a shaper can see** — one whole board, plus the top of the next one, so it is obvious the list
keeps going. Held upright or sideways, on a small phone or a big one, the card is the same fraction
of the screen. Correct in both orientations by construction, which is exactly how the shaper put it.

Everything else about a card stays: the board still draws as a real outline from the same geometry a
tap applies, the name and the four numbers and the descriptor and "Start Shaping" all stay where they
are, and a desktop mouse sees an unchanged card.

Purpose: closes verification gap 2's code half — the fixed 387-dot thumbnail cap the real-device
sweep disproved — implementing the shaper's own 2026-09-11 decision, which supersedes D-08's height.
Output: one changed CSS rule in one shared file, and every card-height test in the suite re-pointed
from a pixel figure nobody can defend to a measured ratio anybody can.
</objective>
