<objective>
Give the home screen back to the boards.

Two things are wrong with it on a phone, and both were measured rather than argued:

**The six design tabs sit at the bottom of the screen while you are still choosing a board.** They
are 56 pixels of navigation to screens you have not picked a board for yet. They go away on the home
screen and come back the moment a board is in hand.

**A preset card is 761 pixels tall on a Pixel 7** — taller than the screen. Four boards cost more
than four screens of scrolling, and almost all of that height is the board drawing. Capping the
drawing's box at 387 pixels brings a card to 550, so a shaper sees a board and the top of the next
one, and the board still draws about 357 pixels long — long enough that a fish still reads as a fish.
The founder chose this height from screenshots, over a shorter card that turned the boards into
slivers and over a sideways board that was measured and declined.

Both fixes are CSS and one route check. No geometry changes, no viewer option, no numbers move, and
a desktop mouse sees byte-identical cards.

The two card files draw the same thumbnail from near-identical copied markup, so the cap is written
once in a shared piece both import — the drift D-02 exists to close off.

Purpose: D-01, D-02, D-07 and D-08 — picking a board on a phone stops being a scrolling exercise.
Output: one route rule, one shared thumbnail component, two cards pointed at it, an inverted
home-screen test and a new sideways-phone test.
</objective>
