<objective>
**Make the app stop mistaking a phone on its side for a desktop.**

The app decides "this is a phone" by one number: is the screen narrower than 820 dots. A real
iPhone held sideways is about 844 dots wide, so at the exact moment its screen is shortest, the app
decides it is looking at a desktop and hands it the desktop layout. Three of the four failures the
shaper found on real phones trace back to that single mistake:

- the board card grew to 757 dots tall on a 237-dot-tall screen, because the card's height cap is
  switched on by that same rule and therefore switched off sideways;
- the "Hide Toolbar" tip — built in Phase 9 for exactly this, and already worded correctly —
  disappeared sideways, the one moment a shaper wants the extra height;
- the row of six screen tabs jumped from the bottom of the screen to the top bar when the phone was
  turned, which is not what a shaper expects from turning a phone.

The fix the shaper chose: **a phone held sideways stays a phone.** The switch becomes *narrower than
820 dots* **or** *a touch screen shorter than 500 dots*. Deliberately not "any touch device" — that
would newly turn an iPad and a touch laptop into phones, which nobody asked for. 500 is not a new
number either: it is already what this app means by "a short screen", the rule that decides where
the FINS Base Length key sits.

It is written once, in one file, and about 118 places in the app inherit it without being touched.
That is also why it is the riskiest change in this set, and why it lands first and alone, proved
end to end, before anything is built on top of it.

Purpose: closes verification gaps 4 and 5 and code-review finding WR-03 — the disproven
sideways-phone assumption, the CLAUDE.md line that records it as fact, and the test comment that
repeats it.
Output: one CSS switch, one compiled-CSS proof of it, four browser specs re-pointed at devices where
what they prove is still true, and the project's own Layout documentation made honest.
</objective>
