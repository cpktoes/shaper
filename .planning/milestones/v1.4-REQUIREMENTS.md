# Milestone v1.4 Requirements: Foil the Way a Shaper Cuts It

**Status:** Locked 2026-09-26 from the founder's written brief as the phase's spec ([12-SPEC.md](v1.4-phases/12-foil-the-way-a-shaper-cuts-it/12-SPEC.md)) — six requirements quoted from the brief and five constraints carried from CLAUDE.md and Phase 11. No REQUIREMENTS.md was written for this one-phase milestone; the spec's eleven numbered requirements are the record, and [12-VERIFICATION.md](v1.4-phases/12-foil-the-way-a-shaper-cuts-it/12-VERIFICATION.md) traces every one of them to code and passing tests, with plan 12-10's production step verified in its addendum.
**Phase:** 12 (continuing from v1.3, which ended at Phase 11)
**Decisions:** D-01–D-20 in [12-CONTEXT.md](v1.4-phases/12-foil-the-way-a-shaper-cuts-it/12-CONTEXT.md); the UI contract in [12-UI-SPEC.md](v1.4-phases/12-foil-the-way-a-shaper-cuts-it/12-UI-SPEC.md)

**Goal:** A shaper's board is cut from its blank the way a planer actually works: a constant deck skin comes off first, parallel to the blank's deck; the centre thickness sets the board's bottom under that deck, and the gap down to the blank's bottom reads as the foam to remove and as planer passes; the board's bottom parallels the blank's rocker so the four rocker numbers are the blank's own and each 12" station's thickness falls out of it; the tips are thinned last, only inside the last 12", with the deck pinned or the bottom pinned — the curve through the 12" stations fixed before thinning and still nudged by the fine-tune adjusters. Every printed number stays a number a shaper would cut to, and every board saved under the Phase 11 model still opens.

## Notes

- **One reversal at discussion (D-04):** the founder first asked for Tip Style as an account default only, then reversed it when asked whether a saved board should remember the choice it was cut with — so it is both a default and a per-board setting, carried by the version-5 snapshot.
- **Rulings on measured numbers, not guesses (D-14, D-15, D-16):** the research counted that about 65% of Phase 11 boards would open flagged under the carry-over, that a tip setting exceeds the parallel cut in 9–31% of blank × setting combinations, and that the one-pass-at-placement rule fails 1 in 1,953 listed placements; the founder accepted each outcome as stated.
