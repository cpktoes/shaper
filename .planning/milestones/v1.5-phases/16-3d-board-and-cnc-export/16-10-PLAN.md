<objective>
Stand the board's fins on it: simple, generic fins at their FINS placement, one plain outline per role with a true
base, and a Fins tick box on the panel to hide them.

The fins are pure geometry first (`lib/geometry/fin-outline.ts`): the research's generic outline (§M), parametrised by
its base alone, so a smaller fin is a shorter base and never a shrunk fin (the founder's rule, D-21); the depth per
role from D-25; a single fin as deep as the board is long in feet, read as inches (D-22), on a base 0.70 of its depth,
centred in FINS' box. `placedFins` stands each one on its FINS line with the toe FINS already worked out, square to the
rocker where it stands. The scene extrudes and draws them; the panel's SHOW section gains its first tick box. The files
never hold fins (D-16, proved in 16-12).

Purpose: R3 (the board's realistic look), R5 (fins as a switch), R10, R12. Decisions D-06, D-17, D-21, D-22, D-25.
Output: the fin maths and tests, the units helper, the fins in the scene, the Fins tick, the spec, pictures.
