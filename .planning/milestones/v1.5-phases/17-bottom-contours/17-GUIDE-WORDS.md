# Phase 17: the GUIDE's words — first drafts for the founder's pen

**Written:** 2026-11-08, copied word for word from sketch 021's `GUIDE` and `HOW` tables (the page the founder approved:
*"The Guide looks awesome. Love everything about it."*). Every card on the BOTTOM page's GUIDE tab carries three things:
**what it is and looks like** (in ink), **what it does** (muted) and, under a dashed rule, **How to shape** in the shop's
words. All three are first drafts from shaping lore, never the founder's own — this page exists so he can mark them up.

**How to use it:** read a card, and for any line that is wrong, too long or not how the shop says it, write the line as it
should read. Edits can arrive as a marked-up copy of this file, as a message ("Panel Vee, what it does: …"), or at the
phase's UAT on the live page. The plans build with the drafts as they stand and carry his corrections in as one task;
a card he leaves alone ships as written.

The founder asked for *"VERY briefly (like 1-2 sentences)"* — every card below is two sentences plus the note.

**Metric (the founder, 2026-11-09 — "15 cm in Metric"):** four How to shape notes carry a `6"` — Flat Entry, Deep
Single Concave, Shallow Single Concave and Channels. An Imperial shaper reads `6"` exactly as written below; a Metric
shaper reads `15 cm` in its place. It is a rule-of-thumb spacing said in prose, not a number off the board, so it is the
round `15 cm`, never `15.2 cm`. The lines below stay as written, in inches; the screen swaps that one figure and nothing
else, and a test holds both readings.


## Nose

### 1. Flat Entry

- **What it is and looks like:** The planed bottom left alone from the nose back.
- **What it does:** Neutral and quick to plane; the work is left to the center.
- **How to shape:** Nothing to cut: plane the bottom true and check it with a straightedge laid rail to rail every 6".

### 2. Rolled Belly Entry

- **What it is and looks like:** The bottom rounds from the stringer to the rails under the nose, like a boat's hull.
- **What it does:** Softens the entry in chop and lets the board roll into a turn, at the cost of some speed.
- **How to shape:** Pencil Start and End on the rails. Roll the planer out from the stringer toward each rail in overlapping passes, most at the Deepest station, and screen it until a flexible batten touches everywhere — no flat left between stringer and rail.

### 3. Teardrop Nose Concave

- **What it is and looks like:** A single concave widest under the nose, closing to a point before the center.
- **What it does:** A noserider's lift: it holds air and water under the nose so it rides high with you standing on it.
- **How to shape:** Pencil the edge at Off Rail from the tip back and close it to a point at End. Hollow along the stringer in shallow planer passes from Start, deepest at Deepest, then blend out to the pencil line with a flexible pad; a straightedge across the nose shows the depth.

### 4. Blended Nose Concave

- **What it is and looks like:** A shallow single under the nose that fades into the center without an edge.
- **What it does:** Lifts the entry of a longboard or mid-length without the teardrop's drag.
- **How to shape:** As the teardrop, but with no edge: carry the hollow out to the pencil line with a soft pad so no step is left, and fade it to nothing by End.

### 5. Slight Single Concave Entry

- **What it is and looks like:** The faintest single concave from the nose back, barely a gauge mark deep.
- **What it does:** Quickens the entry and leads the water into the center's concave.
- **How to shape:** A few shallow planer passes along the stringer from Start, screened out to Off Rail — a straightedge should show the gap you set and no more.

### 6. Reverse Vee Nose

- **What it is and looks like:** A vee under the nose — the stringer low, both rails lifted — flattening out toward the center.
- **What it does:** Lets the nose knife through chop and roll into a turn instead of catching.
- **How to shape:** Pencil Start on the rails. Plane each side from the stringer out to the rail, most at the Deepest station and nothing at the stringer, and fade both panels to flat by End; a straightedge laid across should rock on the stringer by the lift you set.

## Center

### 7. Flat Center

- **What it is and looks like:** Nothing cut between the wide point and the fins.
- **What it does:** Fast and predictable; the classic partner to a vee or a double in the tail.
- **How to shape:** Nothing to cut: plane true, and check with a straightedge rail to rail.

### 8. Deep Single Concave

- **What it is and looks like:** One trough across the center, deepest at the stringer, running out flat at the rails.
- **What it does:** Lift and drive: it sends the water straight down the board. The modern shortboard standard.
- **How to shape:** Pencil the edge at Off Rail from Start to End. Hollow along the stringer in shallow planer passes, deepest at the Deepest station, then screen out to the edge with a flexible pad; check the gap with a straightedge every 6" and keep both sides the same.

### 9. Shallow Single Concave

- **What it is and looks like:** The same single at half the depth.
- **What it does:** Some of the lift with less of the drag, and friendlier in bumpy water.
- **How to shape:** The same cut with half the passes — most of it is in the screening. Check it with a straightedge every 6".

### 10. Early Double Concave

- **What it is and looks like:** Two troughs either side of a flat at the stringer, starting ahead of the wide point.
- **What it does:** Splits the water early, loosens the board, and runs on into a tail double or vee.
- **How to shape:** Pencil the flat at Off Stringer, the trough line at Trough At and the edge at Off Rail. Hollow each side along its trough line with a block or a small plane, leaving the stringer flat untouched, and blend both ways; a straightedge across shows the two gaps and the ridge.

### 11. Continuous Belly / Rolled Mid

- **What it is and looks like:** The bottom rounds from the stringer to the rails along the whole middle — a displacement hull.
- **What it does:** Glides through chop and rolls into turns, giving up speed and lift.
- **How to shape:** Roll the planer out from the stringer to the rails in long overlapping passes from Start to End, most at Deepest, then screen until a flexible batten touches everywhere; no flat anywhere between the stringer and the rail.

### 12. Tri-Plane Hull Center

- **What it is and looks like:** A flat panel down the stringer with a flat plane each side, angled up to the rails.
- **What it does:** Fast off the panel, with a vee's ease rail to rail.
- **How to shape:** Pencil the panel's edge at Off Stringer each side. Plane each outer plane flat from that line out to the rail at one angle, most at the rail, leaving the center panel untouched; check each plane with a straightedge along the board and the angle across.

## Tail

### 13. Flat Exit

- **What it is and looks like:** Nothing cut from the fins back.
- **What it does:** The cleanest release; the fins do the turning.
- **How to shape:** Nothing to cut: plane true, and check with a straightedge rail to rail.

### 14. Double Concave Exit

- **What it is and looks like:** Two troughs through the fins to the tail, a flat left at the stringer.
- **What it does:** Each fin sits in its own stream of water for drive, and the ridge at the stringer helps the board roll rail to rail.
- **How to shape:** Pencil the stringer flat, the trough lines and the edges from DATA's marks. Hollow each side along its trough with a block or a small plane, deepest at the front fins, leaving the stringer flat as the ridge, and fade both out by End.

### 15. Panel Vee

- **What it is and looks like:** Two flat planes meeting at the stringer and rising to the rails, from about the fins out the tail.
- **What it does:** The classic: lifted rails roll into a turn easily, trading away some speed.
- **How to shape:** Pencil Start on the rails. Plane each panel flat from the stringer out to the rail, most at the Deepest station and nothing at the stringer; a straightedge laid across should rock on the stringer by the lift you set, the same both sides.

### 16. Spiral Vee

- **What it is and looks like:** A double concave cut into a vee; the vee grows toward the tail as the double runs out, so the panels seem to twist.
- **What it does:** The double's drive through the fins with the vee's easy roll off the tail.
- **How to shape:** Cut the vee first as a panel vee, then hollow the two concaves into its panels along the trough lines, deepest at the fins, and fade both by End.

### 17. Rolled Vee

- **What it is and looks like:** A vee whose planes are rounded instead of flat, with no corner at the stringer.
- **What it does:** Softer than a panel vee: forgiving through a turn, a touch slower.
- **How to shape:** Cut it as a panel vee, then screen the corner at the stringer and the panels round until a flexible batten rolls over them with no flat and no ridge.

### 18. Extending Single Concave

- **What it is and looks like:** The center's single concave carried through the fins and out the tail.
- **What it does:** Drive and lift to the very end — fast, and stiffer rail to rail.
- **How to shape:** Carry the center's edge line through the fins to End and hollow along the stringer the same way, deepest at the front fins; fade it to flat by End so the tail releases.

## Channels (inside TAIL, cut last)

### 19. Channels

*Reworded 2026-11-10 after the channels' Run got its Angle (D-44) — the founder: "I suppose the guide for along rail*
*channels needs to be updated." The three ways to lay them are named, the layout note says DATA's marks carry the Run*
*and its Angle, and "straight along the board" became "a straight line from mark to mark". A first draft like the rest.*

- **What it is and looks like:** Four, six or eight troughs through the fins, each a ramp into a vertical wall at the rail side — laid Parallel to the stringer, fanned With the rail or turned Toe-in with the fins — and cut last.
- **What it does:** Every wall grips like a small fin: drive and hold at speed in clean, powerful waves.
- **How to shape:** Rule every wall off the stringer at each 6" station from DATA's marks and join the marks with a straightedge — the marks already carry the Run and its Angle. Cut each channel from its wall with a small plane or a block, the floor ramping up to the next peak and the wall square to the bottom, deepest at the front fins; sand the floors with paper on a dowel and keep every wall a straight line from mark to mark.
