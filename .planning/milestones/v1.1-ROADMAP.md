## Overview

Every board Shaper stores has always been in millimetres — the imperial numbers on screen were a
presentation layer over metric data. v1.1 gave the shaper a choice about that layer: pick Imperial
or Metric from the gear menu, and roughly 300 numbers across about 25 files follow, without a
single stored value changing.

Metric here means all-metric, and it splits into two families decided by what a number *is*, not by
which screen it is on. **Dims** — a board's length, widths and headline thickness, the numbers a
shaper quotes as a size — read in centimetres to one decimal. **Marks** — rail band marks, rocker
heights, foil station thicknesses and every fin placement figure — read in whole millimetres,
because that is what a metric tape actually reads for the small stuff. Litres are litres in both.

It shipped in three passes a shaper could try one at a time: the chooser itself proving the chain
end to end on the setup screen, then the five design screens, then everything that comes out of a
printer.

## Milestone Summary

**Decimal Phases:** none — phases 5, 6 and 7 ran as planned, with no urgent insertions.

**What Was Built**

1. **A units chooser that follows the shaper, not the browser.** Imperial/Metric sits beside the
   theme chooser in the gear menu. Signed in, the choice is saved on the account and waiting on any
   other device; signed out, the browser remembers it alone. Signing in either adopts the account's
   saved choice or promotes the browser's when the account has none — reconciled silently, with no
   flash of the wrong system, because the server resolves it before the first paint.
2. **One display boundary, mechanically enforced.** `lib/geometry/units.ts` gained the metric side
   of the number rules — centimetre and whole-millimetre formatting, decimal-cm and whole-mm
   parsing, and the round-trip guarantee — beside the imperial functions, pure and unit-tested.
   `lib/geometry/measure-display.ts` became the single boundary every screen and printer reads
   through.
3. **All five design screens in metric.** Outline, rails, fins, rocker and volume: every slider,
   typed field, viewer callout and data table reads and accepts the chosen system, with sliders
   landing on whole millimetres rather than between them. A shaper can flip systems mid-design and
   the board itself never moves.
4. **Everything a printer produces.** The Summary order form (seven dimension cells each carrying
   their own unit, fin placement in millimetres), the Overview Sheet, the Full Sized Template and
   the Paper Saver. Registration lines read `914 mm from tail — rail 273 mm`; the scale-check square
   stays exactly two inches in both systems but is captioned `50.8 mm` in Metric, so a metric ruler
   alone confirms a true 1:1 print.
5. **A ledger that fails when someone forgets.** `lib/units-isolation.test.ts` walks the
   design-screen and print-surface directories and fails the suite the moment a display file is
   added without being wired to the chooser. A new screen, or a new printed measurement, cannot
   quietly stay in inches.

**Key Decisions**

- **Metric means all-metric, including length** — no hybrid "centimetres for width, feet for
  length" mode. Rationale: a shaper working in metric works in metric.
- **Two number families, decided by what a number is** (dims in cm to one decimal, marks in whole
  mm) rather than by which screen it appears on. Rationale: matches what a metric tape reads.
- **The preference is display-only and lives outside a saved board.** It sits on its own
  `user_preferences` table, never on `models`. Rationale: there is no such thing as "per-board
  units", and switching systems can never rewrite a saved board.
- **The printed scale-check square stays two inches in both systems**, with only its caption
  changing. Rationale: it is a calibration reference measured with a ruler, so it must agree with
  what is actually drawn — this is why its metric caption is the app's one millimetre value that
  carries a decimal (`50.8 mm`).
- **A unit is carried once per line of running text; a value standing alone in its own box carries
  its own.** Rationale: the Full Sized Template's wrapped dims row and the order form's seven
  bordered cells are the same numbers formatted two different ways, on purpose.

**Issues Resolved During The Milestone**

- The typed board-length box clipped its own value in Metric (G-06-4) — character count had been
  used as a proxy for pixel width. Widened to measured widths; the rocker datasheet's cells kept
  byte-identical.
- Fin placement numbers read in centimetres when they should have read in whole millimetres
  (G-06-12, G-06-15) — the fin model learned which of its numbers are lengths and which are marks.
- A code review caught a bug six green waves and a 5/5 verifier pass had missed: slider bounds were
  in millimetres while the typed-value path clamped in centimetres, so a typed board length could
  have stored a 15-metre board. Fixed, with an integration test that wires the real component path.

**Issues Deferred**

- The order form's page-2 rounding note (`All measurements round to the nearest 1/16" …`) is a
  Phase 1 literal in `lib/geometry/fins.ts`, outside the display boundary, and does not follow the
  chooser. Logged in `07-UAT.md` rather than fixed, as out of this milestone's scope.

**Technical Debt Incurred**

- The template name block derives its bare Metric offset by regex-stripping ` cm` off a formatter's
  output instead of calling a dedicated bare formatter (07-REVIEW.md WR-02). Correct today; fragile
  to a future formatting change.
- Nyquist validation was never reconciled: phase 5 has no VALIDATION.md, phases 6 and 7 are still
  `status: draft`. Missing coverage, not a failure.
- No SECURITY.md for phases 5 and 7. Phase 6's is verified with zero threats open. Phase 5 is the
  one worth a retroactive pass, since it added a database table and a server action.
