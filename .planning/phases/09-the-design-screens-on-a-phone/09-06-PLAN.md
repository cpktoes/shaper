<objective>
Decide, in tested arithmetic, which point a thumb has grabbed.

Today each drag point owns its own invisible circle, 15 pixels across the radius, and the browser
decides which one a press lands on by whatever was drawn last. That is fine while the circles never
touch. Make them finger-sized — 44 pixels across — and on a short, wide board they start to overlap,
and the browser's answer stops being the right one. So the rule becomes: the point nearest your
finger wins (D-15). It is the same rule for a mouse, and at the desktop's 15-pixel circles it gives
exactly today's answer, which is what keeps desktop dragging untouched.

Because the calculators are the product, this decision is arithmetic and belongs in `lib/geometry/`
with tests, beside the solvers it feeds — it decides which number on the board a gesture changes.

And the radius itself is measured, not guessed: this plan works out how far apart the outline's five
points and the rocker's four handles actually land on a phone screen, on the tightest board a shaper
can build, before writing a number down.

Purpose: PHON-04's correctness, settled in pure code before any pointer handler is touched.
Output: two pure pick functions with tests, two measured hit radii, and a committed measurement that
fails if the geometry ever drifts underneath them.
</objective>
