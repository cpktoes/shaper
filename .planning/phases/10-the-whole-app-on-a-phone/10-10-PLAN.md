<objective>
**A phone on its side goes back to being a normal browser.**

On 2026-09-11 the shaper walked a real phone and reached the design screens sideways. The verdict
was blunt: *"horizontal is useless, as there are no controls other than the drag. It was honestly
better when it was treated as a normal browser rather than a phone. I did not see the hide toolbar
tip."*

So the change that made a sideways phone keep the phone layout comes back out. The reason it was
wrong is simple once you hold the phone: the desktop layout needs 820 dots because that is 340 dots
of controls beside a 480-dot drawing, and a phone on its side is about 844 to 863 dots. It fits.
Controls beside the board is a much better use of a short wide screen than a half-height drawing
stacked on top of controls with almost nothing left under them.

One thing does not simply revert with it. The tip that tells a shaper how to hide Safari's own
toolbar was the single symptom that change genuinely existed to fix — and it still did not appear.
It was hiding behind the layout switch, and it never belonged there: whether a browser has a toolbar
worth hiding is a question about the device in your hand, not about which layout the screen picked.
So the tip moves onto the axis it actually belongs on, and appears on an iPhone held either way.
On an Android phone it still does not appear, and that has always been correct — its advice is
Safari's own menu wording.

Everything that was rewritten to describe the withdrawn rule gets rewritten again to describe the
one that exists: the project's own Layout notes, the switch's own comment, four doc comments, and
the four tests that were moved onto a tablet because a sideways phone had stopped being a valid
place to prove what they prove.

Purpose: closes fixes 1 and 4 of the four the 2026-09-11 walk produced — D-10, and the tip that was
never delivered.
Output: one layout switch that asks one question, a tip that shows up when it is wanted, and a set
of notes and tests that say what is true.
</objective>
