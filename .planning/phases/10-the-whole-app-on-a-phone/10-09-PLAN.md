<objective>
**Make sure a shaper can always see the board.**

A real phone came back on 2026-09-11 with one blunt sentence: *"horizontal the boards on the
landing page cards have a 0 height."* The code agrees. A board card's picture is capped at
three-quarters of what a shaper can see, minus a fixed amount for the card's own text — and that
text does not shrink when the screen does. On a short screen it eats the whole allowance: about 88
dots of board left at a 390-dot screen, 35 at 320, 5 at 280, and nothing at all at 270 and under.

That is not a wrong number. It is a formula that is allowed to reach zero, and the fix is to stop
it being able to. The picture gets a floor: a smallest height it may never go below, whatever the
screen.

Two other things change with it, both for the same reason. The cap stops asking "which layout is
this screen using?" and starts asking "is a finger driving this?" — because how big a picture of a
board draws is a sizing question, and this project's own rule is that width picks the layout and
the pointer picks how big a thing draws. And the whole sum moves into `app/globals.css` beside the
two numbers it already reads, so there is one place the card's height is decided rather than a
piece of arithmetic in one file depending on measurements written in another.

Everything else about a card stays exactly as it is. The board still draws as a real outline from
the same geometry a tap applies. The name, the four numbers, the descriptor and "Start Shaping" all
stay where they are. A desktop mouse sees an unchanged card at every width — and this plan proves
that rather than claiming it.

Purpose: closes fix 2 of the four the 2026-09-11 walk produced — the confirmed defect in plan 10-06.
Output: one named floor and one named cap expression in `app/globals.css`, one class in the shared
card picture, and three browser tests that measure the result instead of trusting the sum.
</objective>
