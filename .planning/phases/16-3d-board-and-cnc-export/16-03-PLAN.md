<objective>
Build the 3D board's surface as pure, tested maths: the litres' own cross-sections lofted along the rocker into one
closed mesh, with the swallow's two pieces, the tips closed, the hard edge's fade, the sharp edges kept sharp, and the
curves the CNC files will carry.

There is no screen in this plan. `lib/geometry/board-surface.ts` arranges what `boardSections` already computes — no
new section maths (D-05) — into rings, cuts them at the stringer (or at a swallow's notch wall), resamples each into
deck / rail / bottom runs so the bottom edge always lands on the same ring index, lofts and caps them, and checks the
result: closed, consistently wound, no zero-area triangles, and its enclosed volume within (−0.15%, 0] of VOLUME's
litres on all 40 test boards (research §F, measured worst −0.098%). The slices for the files are the litres' own
stations, literally rings of the mesh (D-24). `board-surface-render.ts` gives the screen its normals with the bottom
edge kept sharp by topology, not by a crease angle (Pattern 4), and the stringer's lines. The founder sees the real
surface drawn under the mock-up's lights.

Purpose: R2 (one surface with the litres), R3 (sharp edges and the stringer), R8 (the curves the files hold), R12.
Decisions D-05, D-07, D-09, D-20, D-24, D-26, D-27.
Output: the two modules and their tests; pictures of the Fish and the Shortboard.
</objective>
