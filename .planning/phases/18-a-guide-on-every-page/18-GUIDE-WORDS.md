# Phase 18 — the GUIDE's words, every page

**Source of truth for the words on every GUIDE card** (Phase 18, from sketch 022 as decided 2026-11-09). The code's words module
is tested against this page line for line, as Phase 17's `bottom-guide-words.test.ts` reads `17-GUIDE-WORDS.md`: a correction lands
here and in the module in one change. **Every sentence and How to shape note is a first draft from shaping lore, for the founder's
pen** — except FINS' *Model info* notes and its Convention card, which are the live MODEL INFO tab's own words, carried over verbatim.

Each card: **name** (as the page labels the control) — group — then *is* (what it is and looks like; drawn in ink), *does* (what it does to
the board or the ride; drawn muted after *is* in the same paragraph), *info* (FINS only: the MODEL INFO note, under a Model info label),
*how* (the How to shape note under the dashed rule). Figures in the words are written the Imperial way; Rule 2 says how a Metric shaper
reads the few that carry a unit (noted where they occur). Card names that follow the board (FINS' *Fin Base Length* / *Fin Box Length*)
are noted too. Scraped from the sketch on the Shortboard preset, Imperial, 2026-11-10.

## TEMPLATE

Tabs: VIEWER · ADD WINGS · GUIDE. 22 cards.

*Intro under the GUIDE heading (the board's name filled in):* What each control on this page is, what it does to the board, and how it is shaped. Every card is your Shortboard's outline the way VIEWER draws it, nose left — the whole board, then the part the control shapes, closer, with the setting pushed in muted dashes. The tail shapes are each drawn on this board; the wing cards draw the board with the wing the WINGS tab would add first.

### Board Length

**Board Length** · tag on the Shortboard: `6'2"` · caption: `dashed · 6'8"`  
- is: How long the board is, tip to tip along the stringer — the number every other template number hangs from.
- does: A longer board paddles and glides more and holds a line; a shorter one turns quicker and fits a tighter pocket. The 12" stations and the wide point move with it.
- how: Mark the length on the stringer and square both tips; every station is measured from the tail tip, so get that mark first.

### Nose Controls

**Nose Angle** · tag on the Shortboard: `50°` · caption: `dashed · 70°`  
- is: How sharp the nose comes to its point: pointy at 35°, round at 90°.
- does: A pointy nose cuts into a steep face and trims the swing weight; a round nose carries foam forward for paddling and noseriding.
- how: Trace the template to the nose and fair the last foot with a long batten; sand the tip to the angle by eye against the drawing, keeping both sides the same.

**Fullness** · tag on the Shortboard: `20%` · caption: `dashed · 50% full`  
- is: How much the curve bellies out between the wide point and the nose — thin keeps it straight, full pushes the width forward.
- does: Fullness up front adds paddle and planing area ahead of your chest; thinner keeps the entry rail curved and easier to set.
- how: Spring a batten through the wide point and the nose mark and check the curve has no flat spot; fullness is set in the template, not after cutting.

### Widepoint Controls

**Width** · tag on the Shortboard: `18 3/4"` · caption: `dashed · 19 3/4" wide`  
- is: The board's full width at its widest point.
- does: Wider floats, paddles and planes earlier and is steadier under foot; narrower rolls rail to rail quicker and holds a steeper face.
- how: Mark half the width each side of the stringer at the wide point station and spring the batten through it; a true wide point reads the same both sides.

**Offset** · tag on the Shortboard: `-1"` · caption: `dashed · +2"`  
- is: Where the wide point sits along the board: at the centre, or slid toward the nose (+) or the tail (−).
- does: Forward carries the width into the front half for paddle and trim; back tightens the nose and loads the tail for drive and pivot.
- how: Measure the wide point station from the tail tip before marking the width; the curve either side follows from where this mark lands.

**Tail Rail** · tag on the Shortboard: `50%` · caption: `dashed · 80% long`  
- is: How far the tail's rail runs straight before it curves in — short bends early, long keeps the width back.
- does: A long tail rail holds the width into the tail for speed and planing; a short one pulls the tail in for a tighter turning arc.
- how: Lay a straightedge along the tail rail and mark where it leaves the straight; trim the template so the curve starts there and runs clean to the tail.

**Nose Rail** · tag on the Shortboard: `50%` · caption: `dashed · 80% long`  
- is: How far the nose's rail runs before it curves to the tip.
- does: A long nose rail carries width forward and straightens the entry; a short one lets the nose curve in sooner.
- how: As the tail rail: a straightedge along the nose rail shows where the curve begins; fair the template so it bends there and nowhere else.

### Tail Controls

**Pin**  
- is: A tail that closes to a point, no block.
- does: The least area in the tail: holds in big, steep surf and sinks easily into a turn, but gives up planing speed in small waves.
- how: Cut the outline to a point on the stringer, then round the tip with a block so the glass can wrap it.

**Round**  
- is: A tail that closes in one continuous curve, no corners.
- does: Smooth and forgiving rail to rail; carries more area than a pin and releases softly.
- how: Cut the curve through and fair it with a batten so no flat or corner is left anywhere around the tail.

**Diamond**  
- is: A tail cut to a point behind two corners, the diamond's depth setting how far the point runs back.
- does: The corners give a release point like a squash while the point keeps the outline shorter and the rail line clean.
- how: Mark the block width and the depth, cut the two straight edges to the point, and keep the corners crisp with a block.

**Squash** · tag on the Shortboard: `On this board`  
- is: A tail cut square across a block, the corners rounded.
- does: The planing area and two hard release points of most modern shortboards: fast in small waves, quick off the top.
- how: Mark the block width across the tail, cut square, and round the two corners to a small radius with a block.

**Swallow**  
- is: A tail cut into two points with a crotch between them.
- does: Each tip holds like a pin while the width between them keeps the planing speed — the fish's tail.
- how: Mark the block width and the crotch depth on the stringer, cut the V with a saw and clean it with a file, then round the two tips.

**Tail Block** · tag on the Shortboard: `4"` · caption: `dashed · 6" block`  
- is: The width of the tail across its block, on a squash, diamond or swallow.
- does: A wider block means more area and lift in the tail; narrower tightens the turn and the bite.
- how: Mark half the block each side of the stringer at the tail and cut across; check the width with a square before rounding the corners.

**Depth** · tag on the Shortboard: `Diamond & swallow only` · caption: `dashed · as a swallow, 3" deep`  
- is: Only a diamond or swallow tail has a depth: how far the cut runs in — the diamond's point behind its corners, the swallow's crotch between its tips.
- does: On a pin, round or squash tail the slider is greyed out. Deeper makes each tip more its own pin and loosens the tail; shallower keeps more of the block's area working.
- how: On a diamond or swallow, mark the depth on the stringer from the tail and saw the cut in to the mark; a file squares the inside corners.

**Tail Angle** · tag on the Shortboard: `55°` · caption: `dashed · 80°`  
- is: How sharp the tail curve comes to the block or point: pointy at 30°, round at 90°.
- does: A pointier tail pulls the outline in through the fins for hold; a rounder one keeps width behind the fins for speed and release.
- how: Fair the last foot of the tail curve with a batten through the block corners; the angle sets itself once the batten lies clean.

**Fullness** · tag on the Shortboard: `45%` · caption: `dashed · 75% full`  
- is: How much the curve bellies out between the wide point and the tail.
- does: Fuller carries width into the tail for planing and drive; thinner straightens the tail rail for a cleaner line and a tighter arc.
- how: Spring a batten through the wide point and the tail block and look for flats; adjust the template before cutting.

### Wings — the WINGS tab's own sidebar

**+ Add a wing** · tag on the Shortboard: `None yet` · caption: `dashed · a 3/16" wing at 11"`  
- is: A step in the outline ahead of the fins where the rail jumps in toward the stringer — up to two.
- does: The step lets the tail behind it run narrower and release while the width ahead keeps planing; the corner gives the rail a bite point.
- how: Cut the outline to the wingless curve first, mark the wing's station on both rails, then cut the step in with a saw and square it with a block.

**Position from tail** · tag on the Shortboard: `11"` · caption: `dashed · 15" from the tail`  
- is: Where the wing's corner sits along the board, from the tail.
- does: Further back puts the step on top of the fins for a sharper release; further forward shortens the planing tail behind it.
- how: Measure the station from the tail tip on both rails with a square and mark it before cutting; both wings must land at the same station.

**Depth** · tag on the Shortboard: `3/16"` · caption: `dashed · 1/2" deep`  
- is: How far the rail steps in at the wing, toward the stringer.
- does: A deeper wing narrows the tail more and bites harder; a shallow one is a hint of a corner.
- how: Mark the depth in from the rail at the wing's station and cut back to it, checking both sides against the template.

**Vertical run** · tag on the Shortboard: `1/4"` · caption: `dashed · 1 1/2" run`  
- is: How long the step takes along the board: square at zero, a slanted step at two inches.
- does: A square step is a hard corner that releases sharply; a longer run blends the wing into the rail for a softer break.
- how: Mark where the step starts and ends along the rail; cut the slant between them with a saw and fair it with a block.

**Corner radius** · tag on the Shortboard: `0"` · caption: `dashed · 7/16" radius`  
- is: How round the wing's corner is, from a sharp point to the largest radius that fits.
- does: Sharp bites and releases; round softens the corner so the water lets go gradually.
- how: Round the corner with a block or a dowel wrapped in paper to the radius; a sharp wing is left as the saw cut it.

**Lock to front fin · Offset from the fin** · tag on the Shortboard: `Locked` · caption: `dashed · +2" from the fin`  
- is: Ties the wing to the front fin's trailing-edge mark, so it follows the fins; the offset sets how far ahead or behind it sits.
- does: A wing at the fins releases water right where the fins hold; move the fins on FINS and the wing moves with them.
- how: Mark the front fin's trailing edge on the rail from the FINS page, measure the offset from it, and cut the wing there.

## ROCKER

Tabs: VIEWER · PLANING · DATASHEET · GUIDE (a phone: VIEWER · TOP · PLANING · DATA · GUIDE). 14 cards.

*Intro under the GUIDE heading (the board's name filled in):* What each control on this page is, what it does to the board, and how it is shaped. Every card is drawn from your Shortboard in its US Blanks 6'3"RP blank, the way VIEWER draws it — the whole board, then the part the control touches, closer, with the setting pushed in muted dashes so you can see which way it moves. The number beside each name is this board's own.

### Inputs

**Center Thickness** · tag on the Shortboard: `2 1/4"` · caption: `dashed · +1/2" at the center`  
- is: The board's thickness at its centre, deck to bottom — the one number the whole foil is scaled to.
- does: More centre means more float and paddle and a steadier, stiffer board; less means a livelier board that sits lower in the water and buries its rail more easily. It also sets how much foam comes off the blank's bottom.
- how: Set it here, then plane the blank's bottom flat until a caliper at the centre reads this number — the PLANING tab counts the passes. Check it again after the deck skin comes off: the centre is measured deck to bottom.

**Nose Tip · Tail Tip** · tag on the Shortboard: `7/16" · 15/16"` · caption: `dashed · +3/8" at each tip`  
- is: How thick the board finishes at its two tips, measured at the very end of the stringer.
- does: A thin nose tip lightens the swing weight and knifes in; a thin tail tip sinks and releases for sharper turns, while a fuller one floats the tail and planes earlier.
- how: Mark each tip's thickness on the stringer, then taper the foam from the Thinning Starts line down to the mark with the planer and a long block, checking with calipers every few inches.

### Blank

**Blank** · tag on the Shortboard: `US Blanks 6'3"RP`  
- is: The foam your board is cut from: a maker's blank with its own rocker, thickness and width printed at five stations. Your board lies inside it, the foam to come off shaded.
- does: Pick a blank and your rocker is its rocker — the nose and tail lift come from the foam, not a slider. A close blank means little planing and the maker's curve under your board; a fat one means more work and more room to change things.
- how: Choose a blank a little longer than the board, with the nose and tail rocker you want to end with, then check its printed rocker on the foam itself — a straightedge on the stringer, tip to tip — before you cut; a real blank can differ from the page by an eighth.

**Placement** · tag on the Shortboard: `centered` · caption: `dashed · slid 9/16" to the nose`  
- is: Where the board sits along its blank: centred, or slid toward the nose or the tail by as much as the blank leaves at each end.
- does: Slide toward the nose and the board takes more of the blank's nose curve — more nose rocker, less tail; slide toward the tail for the opposite. Every rocker number moves, and the thickness under the board with it.
- how: Mark the board's centre on the blank's stringer where you placed it, lay the template from that mark, and measure both tip rockers off a flat bench before you commit to the outline cut.

**Deck Skin** · tag on the Shortboard: `1/8"` · caption: `dashed · 1/2" skin`  
- is: The layer the planer takes off the blank's deck everywhere, the same depth at every station — a pass or two of crust.
- does: A deeper skin sits the board lower in the blank and leaves clean foam under the glass, and brings a little nose and tail with it as the deck comes down; too little leaves the blank's dense skin and its lumps in your deck.
- how: Set the planer shallow and take the deck off in long, overlapping passes tail to nose, then screen it flat; the PLANING tab gives the passes at your Planer Max Depth.

**Customize Rocker** · tag on the Shortboard: `Natural rocker` · caption: `dashed · +1/2"N18"`  
- is: The blank's rocker as you would order it from the maker — more or less at the nose or tail, the bend starting at the centre or a set distance in from the tip.
- does: A bend from the centre lifts the whole curve gently; one that starts 18 inches in kicks the tip up and leaves the middle flat and fast. The board follows the blank, so every rocker number updates.
- how: Order the blank with that note, or on a stock blank check the natural rocker with a straightedge, mark where the bend starts, and plane the extra off the bottom toward the tip in tapering passes; check the new tip height against the DATASHEET.

### Rocker — read off the blank — hand-set until you pick one

**Nose Rocker** · tag on the Shortboard: `4 1/4"` · caption: `dashed · +1" nose rocker`  
- is: How high the nose tip rises above the flat the board lies on, with the 12-inch station saying how the curve gets there.
- does: More nose rocker keeps the nose from pearling on steep drops and loosens the board, at the cost of paddle speed and glide; a flatter nose planes faster and catches waves earlier but can catch on late drops.
- how: Lay the blank bottom-up on a flat bench, set a long straightedge on the stringer's low point and measure up to the nose tip; plane the bottom toward the tip to bring it to the number, checking the 12-inch station on the way.

**Tail Rocker** · tag on the Shortboard: `1 9/16"` · caption: `dashed · +1" tail rocker`  
- is: How high the tail tip rises above the flat, and the 12-inch station that shapes the kick.
- does: More tail rocker pivots tighter and holds in the pocket but drags on the flats; a flatter tail is faster and drives down the line, and takes more effort to turn.
- how: Measure as for the nose, from the straightedge up to the tail tip; the last 12 inches hold the kick, so take the bottom down in short passes and keep the curve continuous — no flat spot under the fins.

### Foil

**Pin tips to · Deck** · tag on the Shortboard: `On this board`  
- is: The tips are thinned from the bottom: the deck keeps the blank's curve and the foam at each tip comes off below it, so the tip rocker rises.
- does: A lifted, fine tip with the deck line untouched — more rocker at the ends than the blank printed, and a flatter deck to stand on.
- how: Pencil the Thinning Starts line across the bottom, then plane the bottom toward each tip, most at the tip and nothing at the line; check the tip thickness with calipers and the new tip rocker against the DATASHEET.

**Pin tips to · Bottom**  
- is: The tips are thinned from the deck: the bottom keeps the blank's rocker exactly and the foam comes off the deck toward each tip.
- does: The blank's rocker to the inch and a deck that rolls down into the tips — the tips' thinness in the deck rather than in the rocker.
- how: Pencil the Thinning Starts line across the deck and plane from it toward each tip, most at the tip and nothing at the line; leave the bottom untouched and check the tip thickness with calipers.

**Nose Thinning Starts** · tag on the Shortboard: `12" · Automatic` · caption: `dashed · starts 24" in`  
- is: Where the nose stops being the planed blank and starts tapering down to the tip thickness — 12 inches from the tip unless the board cannot run down steadily from there, or wherever you set it.
- does: Start it further in and the taper is longer and gentler, the nose lighter and finer; start it near the tip and the board keeps its full foil to within a foot of the nose, fuller and more forgiving.
- how: Pencil a line across the board at the start, then plane from that line toward the tip in tapering passes down to the tip mark; nothing comes off inside the line.

**Tail Thinning Starts** · tag on the Shortboard: `12" · Automatic` · caption: `dashed · starts 24" in`  
- is: Where the tail stops being the planed blank and starts tapering down to the tip thickness — 12 inches from the tip unless the board cannot run down steadily from there, or wherever you set it.
- does: Start it further in for a thinner, more sensitive tail that sinks into turns; near the tip for a fuller tail that floats and planes early.
- how: Pencil a line across the board at the start, then plane from that line toward the tip in tapering passes down to the tip mark; nothing comes off inside the line.

**Nose @ 12" · Tail @ 12"** · tag on the Shortboard: `No tweak` · caption: `dashed · +1/4" at both`  
- is: A fine-tune of the thickness at the two 12-inch stations, up to a quarter inch either way on top of what the blank gives there.
- does: A touch more at the tail 12 adds float under your back foot; a touch less at the nose 12 lightens the entry — without changing the centre or the tips.
- how: Check the 12-inch thicknesses with calipers after planing: a plus means leave that much more at the station, a minus a shallow extra pass there, feathered out over a couple of feet so no step shows.

**Fine tune from** · tag on the Shortboard: `Deck` · caption: `dashed · -1/4" at the tail 12"`  
- is: Which surface a 12-inch fine-tune moves: the Deck, so the rocker stays the blank's, or the Bottom, so the bottom moves and the rocker re-levels.
- does: On the Deck nothing under the board changes; on the Bottom a lower 12-inch station flattens the rocker there and every rocker number can shift.
- how: Take the fine-tune off whichever surface you chose — off the deck with a light pass feathered both ways, or off the bottom with a straightedge along the stringer afterward to confirm what the rocker did.

## RAILS

Tabs: VIEWER · DATA · INSTRUCTIONS · GUIDE. 17 cards.

*Intro under the GUIDE heading (the board's name filled in):* What each control on this page is, what it does to the rail, and how it is shaped. Every card is your Shortboard's own rail the way VIEWER draws it — the board from above with the three stations, then the cross-section at one of them: the bands in their colours, the finished rail in ink, with the setting pushed in muted dashes. Each rail has the same controls; the cards draw the center's, and the tail's where only the tail has the control.

### Inputs

**Import Rocker & Foil Values** · tag on the Shortboard: `On this board`  
- is: Takes each rail's thickness from ROCKER & FOIL — the nose 12", centre and tail 12" thicknesses the foil gives — instead of three sliders here.
- does: Every mark below is worked from the thickness, so the rails follow the foil as you change it; untick it to set the three thicknesses by hand.
- how: Leave it ticked and set the thicknesses on ROCKER; untick only when you want a rail worked from a thickness the foil does not give.

**Board Thickness · Thickness @12"** · tag on the Shortboard: `2 1/4"` · caption: `dashed · 2 1/2" thick`  
- is: The board's thickness at this station, deck to bottom — the number every rail mark is a share of.
- does: A thicker rail carries more foam and float and forgives a clumsy edge; a thinner rail bites and rolls over quicker.
- how: Check the planed thickness with calipers at the 12" stations and the centre before marking the rails; every band mark is laid out from it.

### Nose Rail · Center Rail · Tail Rail — every rail has these; drawn at the center, the tail for Hard Edge

**Deck Profile** · tag on the Shortboard: `2 1/4" · Flat` · caption: `dashed · domed to 1 15/16"`  
- is: How much the deck domes down toward the rail: flat leaves the full thickness out to the rail, heavily domed tapers it to three quarters.
- does: Doming thins the rail without thinning the stringer — a lively, sensitive rail under a thick, floaty board — and sets the Tapered Thickness the marks are worked from.
- how: Pencil the Domed Deck Band mark in from the rail on the deck and plane the deck down from that line to the Tapered Thickness at the rail, checking with calipers.

**Family** · tag on the Shortboard: `med/knifey` · caption: `dashed · boxy`  
- is: The rail's character from boxy to knifey: how much of the thickness is apex.
- does: Boxy is full and forgiving with lots of foam at the edge; knifey is pinched and sharp, quick to bury and quick to release.
- how: Lay out the apex marks from DATA on the rail's side and cut the rail bands to them; a knifey rail takes the bands further, a boxy one leaves more of the side standing.

**Ratio** · tag on the Shortboard: `60/40` · caption: `dashed · 35/65`  
- is: How the rail's thickness splits above and below the apex: 60/40 puts the apex low with more deck above it, 50/50 centres it.
- does: A low apex (more above) turns more of the rail downward for bite and hold; a high apex gives a fuller, more forgiving edge in the water.
- how: Mark the Apex Center up from the bottom on the rail's side at each station and join the marks with a batten; the bands above and below meet on that line.

**Symmetrical** · caption: `dashed · symmetrical`  
- is: Makes the bottom tucks mirror the deck marks, so the rail is the same above and below the apex.
- does: An even rail that rolls the same both ways and is simple to shape; off, the bottom tuck follows the apex instead for a harder working edge.
- how: Mark the bottom tucks from DATA, which now equal the deck marks, and cut the bottom bands to mirror the deck bands.

**Hard Edge** · tag on the Shortboard: `On this board` · caption: `dashed · tucked`  
- is: A sharp corner where the bottom meets the rail, instead of a tuck — tail only.
- does: Water lets go cleanly off a hard edge, so the tail is faster and releases in a turn; a tucked edge holds on and forgives.
- how: Plane the bottom flat right out to the rail through the tail and cut the rail band down to meet it in a corner; keep the corner crisp with a block and no sanding.

### Advanced — each rail's fold

**Scaled from center by** · tag on the Shortboard: `1.00` · caption: `dashed · 0.70`  
- is: Shrinks every mark of a nose or tail rail to a share of what the centre's rule would give — 1.00 is the full rule.
- does: Scaling down keeps the thin ends' bands from running too far across a narrow nose or tail; 1.00 shapes them by the same rule as the centre.
- how: Lay out the nose and tail marks from DATA — they are already scaled — and cut the bands to them rather than carrying the centre's marks to the ends.

**Apex Thickness** · tag on the Shortboard: `11/16"` · caption: `dashed · +1/4"`  
- is: Adds to or takes from the apex band's thickness — how much of the rail's edge is the near-vertical apex.
- does: A thicker apex is a fuller, more forgiving edge; a thinner one is a sharper, more sensitive rail.
- how: Mark Rail Mark 1 and Rail Tuck 1 on the rail's side from DATA and leave the band between them standing when the deck and bottom bands are cut.

**Deck Mark 1** · tag on the Shortboard: `2 1/4"` · caption: `dashed · +1/2"`  
- is: Moves the first deck mark — how far in from the rail the first deck band is laid out.
- does: Further in cuts a wider first band and a flatter deck roll; nearer the rail keeps more deck and a quicker turn-down.
- how: Pencil Deck Mark 1 in from the rail along the deck and cut the first deck band from that line down to Rail Mark 1 with the planer.

**Deck Mark 3** · tag on the Shortboard: `4 1/16"` · caption: `dashed · +1/2"`  
- is: Moves the third deck mark — the farthest the deck bands reach toward the stringer.
- does: Further in blends the deck down over a wider band for a softer, rounder rail; nearer keeps the deck flat for longer.
- how: Pencil Deck Mark 3 in from the rail and cut the second deck band from it down to the first, blending the two bands with a screen.

**Domed Deck Band** · tag on the Shortboard: `5 3/8"` · caption: `dashed · +2"`  
- is: Moves where the domed deck starts dropping from the full thickness toward the rail.
- does: Further in domes more of the deck for a thinner rail under a thicker centre; nearer keeps the deck flat almost to the rail.
- how: Pencil the Domed Deck Band line and plane the deck from it out to the rail in long passes until the rail reads the Tapered Thickness.

**Use Single Tuck** · caption: `dashed · one tuck`  
- is: Cuts the bottom with one tuck band instead of two.
- does: One wider band is quicker to shape and softer in the water; two give a tighter, more controlled tuck.
- how: Pencil one Bottom Tuck mark and cut a single band from it up to Rail Tuck 1, then round it with a screen.

**Bottom Tuck 1** · tag on the Shortboard: `5/16"` · caption: `dashed · +1/4"`  
- is: Moves the first bottom tuck mark — how far in from the rail the first tuck band is laid out.
- does: Further in tucks more of the bottom for a softer, more forgiving edge; nearer keeps the bottom flat out to the edge.
- how: Pencil Bottom Tuck 1 in from the rail on the bottom and cut the first tuck band from it up to Rail Tuck 1.

**Bottom Tuck 3** · tag on the Shortboard: `9/16"` · caption: `dashed · +3/4"`  
- is: Moves the last bottom tuck mark — where the bottom band stops and the flat bottom begins.
- does: Further in rolls more of the bottom into the rail; nearer leaves a flatter, faster bottom.
- how: Pencil Bottom Tuck 3 on the bottom and cut the second tuck band from it to the first; leave the bottom flat inside the line.

**Sharp Bottom Tuck** · caption: `dashed · sharp`  
- is: Leaves the last bottom tuck as a corner instead of blending it round.
- does: A crisp line where the tuck meets the bottom releases water sooner — a touch of hard edge along the whole rail.
- how: Cut the last tuck band but do not round where it meets the flat bottom; a light pass with a block keeps the line straight.

**Tail Rail Blends From · Tail Rail Ends At** · tag on the Shortboard: `18" · 30 5/16"` · caption: `dashed · 24" · 36 5/16"`  
- is: Where the tail's hard edge fades into the tucked rail: it runs sharp from the tail to Blends From, fades, and is fully tucked by Ends At.
- does: A longer hard edge drives further up the rail for speed and release; a shorter one hands over to the tuck sooner for a smoother rail through the turn.
- how: Pencil Blends From and Ends At on the rail from the tail; cut the edge hard behind the first mark and screen it progressively rounder until it is the tucked rail at the second.

## VOLUME

Tabs: ESTIMATE · GUIDE. 8 cards.

*Intro under the GUIDE heading (the board's name filled in):* What each control and figure on this page is, what it does, and how it is checked on a board. Every card is your Shortboard — the plan from above and the center cross-section from RAILS — with the quick estimate's number pushed in muted dashes and the litres it would give. The accurate figure the page quotes is the 51 cross-sections; the three sliders and Board Type belong to the quick estimate.

### Inputs — the three sliders and Board Type show only when Import Designed Board is unticked

**Import Designed Board** · tag on the Shortboard: `On this board`  
- is: Measures the board you drew — its template area and 51 real cross-sections with the RAILS rails — instead of the quick estimate from a board type.

### What the ESTIMATE card reads

**Estimated Volume** · tag on the Shortboard: `29.61 L`  
- is: The board's volume in litres — how much foam is in it.
- does: More litres float you higher for paddling and glide and forgive a flat section; fewer sink into the wave face for hold and let the board turn off the rail. Shapers quote it against your weight and fitness.
- how: Volume is read, not shaped: once the template, foil and rails are set it is what they add up to. To change it, go back to thickness and width first.

**Template Area** · tag on the Shortboard: `1042.8 sq in`  
- is: The board's plan area — the area of the template seen from above.
- does: The area the board planes on: more area, more lift and earlier planing; the litres are this area times the effective thickness.
- how: Trace the finished template onto paper to check it against the board; the area follows the outline and nothing else.

**Length-Weighted Effective Thickness** · tag on the Shortboard: `1 1/2"`  
- is: The thickness the board averages along its length once the rails and the thin tips are counted — the number the area is multiplied by.
- does: Two boards with the same centre thickness differ here by their foil and rails: fuller rails and a thicker tail and nose carry more litres.
- how: Nothing to mark: it falls out of the foil and the rail bands. A thicker tail 12" or fuller rails raise it; a thinner nose lowers it.

### Inputs

**Import Designed Board** · tag on the Shortboard: `On this board`  
- is: Takes the board's length, its tail width at 12" and its tail shape from TEMPLATE — the three numbers every fin position is worked from.
- does: The fins follow the outline as you change it; untick to place fins for a board you know only by those three numbers.

### Convention — how every mark below is measured

**Trailing-edge convention** · tag on the Shortboard: `to 1/16"`  
- is: All positions use the trailing-edge convention: off-tail and off-rail are measured to the trailing edge of the fin base, and toe-in is the leading edge's inward offset from that trailing edge (regardless of fin base length).
- does: Rears are often defined by a trailing edge spread rather than off-tail measurement, and every value is rounded to the nearest 1/16".
- how: Mark the trailing edge first — off the tail along the stringer, squared across, then in from the rail — and lay the leading edge from it with the toe; the base length moves the leading-edge mark, never the trailing edge.

### Single Fin — one box on the stringer

**Single Fin** · caption: `drawn as · Single Fin`  
- is: One fin on the stringer in a long box, set well forward of the tail.
- does: Pivot and glide: a single fin trims and carves long arcs and holds a straight line; it does not want to be pumped.
- info: Single fin box placement comes from an established fixed range rather than a length equation — the default value is in a common working range (4 1/2" to 6") and can be fine-tuned by the forward/aft slider in the advanced menu, rather than solved for.
- how: Mark the box's centre on the stringer at the off-tail distance from DATA and route the box square to the stringer, the length of the box.

### Twin — two rail fins, placed by one of three templates

**Twin** · caption: `drawn as · Twin — Modern/Upright`  
- is: Two fins out by the rails and nothing on the stringer.
- does: Loose and fast: twins skate and release off the tail and love small, soft waves; they slide out when pushed hard.
- info: All twin placements come from established fixed ranges rather than a length equation — they're centered in a working range (Modern = 7" to 8", Keel = 6" to 6 1/2", and Upright+Trailer = at ~10") and can be adjusted by the forward/aft slider in the advanced menu, rather than solved for.
- how: Mark each fin's trailing edge off the tail on the stringer and square across, then measure in from the rail at that mark; mark the leading edge with the toe and join the two for the box.

**Modern/Upright** · caption: `drawn as · Twin — Modern/Upright`  
- is: The modern twin: upright fins by the rails, toed in.
- does: Fast and loose with more drive than a keel; the everyday twin.
- info: Good for shorter base (generally ≤ 5 1/2"), more upright twin fins.
- how: Mark the trailing edges off the tail and in from the rail, then the leading edges with the toe, and join them for the boxes.

**Keel** · caption: `drawn as · Twin — Keel`  
- is: Keel fins: long, low bases set further back with little toe.
- does: The retro fish feel: glide and down-the-line speed, long drawn turns, little pivot.
- info: Use for wide base (generally > 5 1/2"), retro-style twin fins.
- how: As the upright twin, with the keel's longer base: mark both ends of the base and keep the toe small.

**Upright + Trailer** · caption: `drawn as · Twin — Upright + Trailer`  
- is: Upright twins with a small trailer fin behind them on the stringer.
- does: A twin's speed with a touch of hold at the tail, so it releases less when pushed.
- info: Typically used with shorter-base, thruster-like, twins with a small trailer fin for added hold and drive.
- how: Mark the twins as uprights, then the trailer's trailing edge on the stringer four inches off the tail.

### Thruster — the front pair by one of four front models (the quad's front pair uses them too), the center fin behind

**Thruster** · tag on the Shortboard: `On this board` · caption: `drawn as · Thruster — Basic (Half Rate)`  
- is: Two front fins by the rails and a centre fin behind them.
- does: The all-rounder: the centre fin holds what the twins give away, so it drives through turns and pumps for speed.
- how: Mark the two front trailing edges off the tail and in from the rail, their leading edges with the toe, then the centre fin's trailing edge on the stringer.

**Proportional** · caption: `drawn as · Thruster — Proportional`  
- is: Front fins placed at a fixed share of the board's length, so a longer board puts them further up.
- does: A simple rule that keeps the cluster in proportion to the board; a touch further forward than the other models on a long board.
- info: A straightforward linear length-scaled placement based on a smaller range of short board. It's the simplest of the four and the least tuned for longer boards.
- how: Use DATA's marks for this model — off tail, off rail and toe — and check both sides measure the same before routing.

**Basic (Half Rate)** · tag on the Shortboard: `On this board` · caption: `drawn as · Thruster — Basic (Half Rate)`  
- is: Front fins placed by a straight-line rule that moves them up at half the rate of the board's length.
- does: Conservative: the cluster stays nearer the tail as the board grows, for a tighter, more pivoty feel.
- info: Also scales linearly with length, but more slowly. It follows a "half rate convention" based on the idea that stance width scales slower than board length.
- how: Use DATA's marks for this model and check both sides the same; the toe is a quarter inch here.

**McKee Shortboard** · caption: `drawn as · Thruster — McKee Shortboard`

### Advanced — the forward fins'; the rear pair and the center fin have the same

**Fin Base Length** · tag on the Shortboard: `4 1/2"` · caption: `dashed · 5 1/2" base`  
- is: The length of the fin's base along the board — the fin's footprint, trailing edge to leading edge.
- does: A longer base drives and holds more; a shorter one pivots. The leading-edge mark moves with it.
- how: Mark both ends of the base on the board — trailing edge first, then the base length forward along the toe line — and route the box to those marks.

**Aft/Forward position** · tag on the Shortboard: `11"` · caption: `dashed · 1" forward`  
- is: Slides a fin set forward or back from where its model puts it, up to an inch and a half either way.
- does: Back is drivey — the fins hold and the board tracks; forward is loose and quick to pivot.
- how: Move both trailing-edge marks the same amount along the board before marking the leading edges; measure from the tail tip each time.

**Off-Rail** · tag on the Shortboard: `1 3/16"` · caption: `dashed · 7/8" off the rail`  
- is: How far in from the rail the fins' trailing edges sit.
- does: Closer to the rail bites earlier and feels looser; closer to the stringer is more forgiving and drives straighter.
- how: Measure in from the rail at each trailing-edge mark with a rule held square to the stringer; mark both sides and check them against each other.

**Toe-In** · tag on the Shortboard: `1/4"` · caption: `dashed · 1/2" toe-in`  
- is: How much the leading edge points in toward the stringer, as a distance at the leading edge.
- does: More toe-in is looser and turns easier at the cost of speed; less is drivey and fast down the line.
- how: Mark the leading edge the toe distance further in from the rail than the trailing edge, then lay a straightedge through the two marks to pencil the box line.
