<objective>
At his look at 17-21 the founder approved it and answered its three questions. This plan builds all three answers.

**"Draw the concave."** On a board with a tail concave (single or double) and channels, RAILS' tail section today cuts
the channels into a flat bottom and leaves the concave out. Now it draws the concave's own hollow under the channels,
then cuts the channels into it last, straight up at every wall, on both the thin bottom line and the thick Finished
Rail line. The rail RAILS bands does not get thinner — a concave hollows the middle and leaves the rail at the planed
bottom — so the rail thickness, the readout and RAILS' DATA stay exactly as they are. A band mark that stands inside the
concave now sits on the concave's surface, the way marks already sit on a vee: on the Shortboard and the Fish no mark is
inside it at the tail section, so none moves; on the Longboard's rolled rail the inner bottom tuck mark rises by a few
hundredths of an inch. With the channels set to None the concave is still not drawn on RAILS, as he ruled before (D-41).

**"Match DATA."** BOTTOM's cross-section has a gauge at the rail that reads "rail lift". At the very tail of a board whose
outer channel runs out through the rail it was counting the channel — the Fish with a flat tail and eight channels read
5/16" there while DATA now reads a dash. The gauge now reads what DATA reads: the vee's or belly's lift alone. A flat
tail or a concave shows no rail-lift figure at all. Every other gauge on the drawing stays exactly as it is, on screen
and on the printed BOTTOM CONTOURS page.

**"Use the sentence."** DATA's note under the depths table now says, in his words: "Rail lift is the vee's or belly's
lift at the rail; a channel is cut after the rail is shaped and never counts." The printed page's tables sheet carries
the same note.

No litres figure moves anywhere.

channels), R7 (the cross-section and its gauges), R8 (DATA), R11 (RAILS works from what the bottom leaves), R14 (the
