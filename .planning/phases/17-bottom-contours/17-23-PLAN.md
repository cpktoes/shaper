<objective>
At his look at 17-21, and then on the first draft of this work, the founder gave three answers about BOTTOM's numbers
and words. This plan builds all three.

**"Match DATA."** BOTTOM's cross-section has a gauge at the rail that reads "rail lift". At the very tail of a board whose
outer channel runs out through the rail it was counting the channel. The Fish with a flat tail and eight channels read
5/16" there, while DATA now reads a dash. The gauge now reads what DATA reads: the vee's or belly's lift alone. A flat
tail or a concave shows no rail-lift figure at all. Every other gauge on the drawing stays exactly as it is, on screen
and on the printed BOTTOM CONTOURS page.

**"Use the sentence."** DATA's note under the depths table now says, in his words: "Rail lift is the vee's or belly's
lift at the rail; a channel is cut after the rail is shaped and never counts." The printed tables sheet carries the
same note.

**"Make it fit."** That sentence is longer. On the 9'+ Longboard the printed tables sheet then had no room for its
other notes — and with channels it already printed only the first of its three. Now every note prints on the
Longboard's tables sheet, on every setup. Where the notes would not otherwise fit, that sheet's table rows sit two dots
closer together; the type stays the same size. Every other board's sheet is exactly as it was, and no board prints a
page more or fewer.

No litres figure and no DATA figure moves.

2026-11-11: "Match DATA", "Use the sentence", "Make it fit"). Requirements R7 (the cross-section and its gauges), R8
(DATA), R14 (the paper), R15 (shown before it ships). Decisions D-05, D-13, D-25, D-29, D-30, D-40, D-43.
Output: the rail-lift gauge from the vee or belly alone (`bottom-draw.ts`); DATA's note in his words (`bottom-copy.ts`);
