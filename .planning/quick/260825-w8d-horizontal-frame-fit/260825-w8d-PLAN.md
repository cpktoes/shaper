<objective>
Fit the horizontal (rotated) viewBox to what the rotated drawing actually contains, instead of
transposing the vertical frame's dimensions.

Purpose: rotating the board exists to make the board bigger. Measured on the running app at a
1280x820 window, it currently makes it **2% bigger** — 566px vertical to 578px horizontal. That
is not a feature, it is a no-op with a button on it. The frame is the reason: it is built from
the vertical frame's numbers, which are sized for a layout the rotation has already changed.

Output: the same rotation, drawn into a frame sized from the callout system's own constants.
No new behaviour, no new controls, one file.
</objective>

### What is wrong

`components/outline/outline-viewer.tsx` builds the horizontal frame by transposing the vertical
one:

```ts
const horizW = baseH + 2 * HORIZONTAL_END_PAD;   // 638 + 157.58 = 795.58
const horizH = baseW;                            // 514
```

`baseW` is the **vertical** frame's width. That width exists to hold two things that sit side by
side in the vertical layout: a left input-chip gutter and a right output rail. Rotated, those two
rails stack across the short axis far more compactly, so most of the 514 is empty air. And
`HORIZONTAL_END_PAD` pads a full half-chip at each end, ignoring the margin the vertical frame
already carries past the nose and the tail.

### The coordinate mapping (unchanged, from 260825-vot)

The content group draws a -90 degree rotation, which maps `(x, y) -> (y, -x)`:

- canonical **y** becomes the rotated **x** — the LONG axis, along the board
- canonical **x** becomes the rotated **y**, negated — the SHORT axis, across the board

So screen-x = canonical-y, and screen-y = -(canonical-x). A LARGER canonical x is HIGHER on the
rotated screen.

`frame.chipRightX` and `frame.outputValueX` **already carry this board's overflow** (a wide board
pushes both rails outward). Building the short axis from them means a wide board widens the
rotated frame correctly, with no second overflow term.

### The size ceiling, and why it must be a constant

`pinnedCalloutSizes(fitScale)` grows a pinned callout as the fit scale falls and floors that
growth at `MIN_PINNED_FIT_SCALE`. **Evaluating it at the floor gives the ceiling.** A viewer that
does not pin draws `UNPINNED_CALLOUT_SIZES`, so the true bound is the larger of the two:

| | pinned at 0.66 | unpinned | bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| `chipW` | 157.58 | 112 | **157.58** |
| `chipH` | 48.48 | 38 | **48.48** |
| `name` | 16.67 | 12 | **16.67** |
| `value` | 21.21 | 17 | **21.21** |

This must stay a module constant. The frame feeds `useSvgFitScale`, the fit scale produces the
live `calloutSizes`, so sizing the frame from live callout sizes closes that chain into a resize
feedback loop — threat T-VOT-04, already on the record. **A `getBBox()`-then-resize approach has
exactly the same defect** plus a measure-render pass, and is rejected for that reason, not merely
because it is fragile.

The floor is genuinely reachable, so the ceiling is not academic: at the new frame width a fit
scale of 0.66 arrives at a viewer panel about 480 CSS px wide, which is roughly a 750px browser
window — a real tablet and split-screen size.

### The long axis (each end of the board)

Rotated, `CalloutChip` centres its box on the anchor along this axis
(`rectX = x - sizes.chipW / 2`), so the chip at an extreme station overhangs that station by
`chipW / 2` = **78.79** at the ceiling.

The two extreme chips are LENGTH at `lengthChipY = tipPy` = 24, and TAIL BLOCK at
`tailBlockChipY = lenToY(tailPodStationIn)`, which is at most `tailPy` = 596 (the tail pod
station is never negative). But the frame already reaches past both:

- nose: `tipPy - baseMinY` = 24 - (-16) = **40** units of existing margin
- tail: `(baseMinY + baseH) - tailPy` = 622 - 596 = **26** units of existing margin

So the pad each end actually needs is only the shortfall: **38.79** at the nose and **52.79** at
the tail, not 78.79 twice. That takes the long axis from 795.58 to **729.58**.

### The short axis (across the board)

Working in canonical x, then negating:

- **Output side (top of the rotated screen).** `OutputRail` anchors its text at
  `(valueX, y)` and, in horizontal, puts the value line at `y - sizes.name * 1.15` with the
  station line on the anchor baseline. Inside `UprightAt` a displacement in child coordinates is
  a displacement on screen, so the stack reaches `name * 1.15` plus the value's own ascender past
  the anchor. Bounding the ascender by the full font size (conservative, and cheap):
  `16.67 * 1.15 + 21.21` = **40.38**.
- **Chip side (bottom of the rotated screen).** The chip hangs a full `chipH` down from its
  anchor, and WP OFFSET sits a second row further out at `chipH + CHIP_STACK_GAP`:
  `2 * 48.48 + 6` = **102.97**.
- **The board itself** spans `pxX(-wpHalfWidthIn)` to `pxX(wpHalfWidthIn)`, which at these
  constants is 94.5 to 245.5 — comfortably inside both rails, by 139 and 77 units.

So the short axis runs from `frame.chipRightX - 102.97` = -44.97 to
`frame.outputValueX + 40.38` = 322.38: **367.35** units, against the 514 being used today.

Everything else drawn in the callout path is bounded by these: the station lines, the
construction overlay and the widepoint dots are all at `pxX(+/- halfWidth)`; the output extension
line stops at `valueX - CALLOUT_VALUE_GAP`; the chip leader runs from `chipRightX` inward; the
drag rings reach about 7 CSS px past the board edge, against a 36.5-unit gutter gap.

### What this buys, honestly

The viewer panel is 804 x 631 at a 1280x820 window (aspect 1.274), and it does **not** size itself
from the frame — that was tried and reverted, see the comment in `outline-editor.tsx`. So the
container is fixed and `preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"` gives
`scale = min(804 / horizW, 631 / horizH)`.

| | horizW | horizH | aspect | bound by | scale | board |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped | 795.58 | 514 | 1.548 | width | 1.011 | 578px |
| this plan | 729.58 | 367.35 | 1.986 | width | 1.102 | **~630px** |
| content-tight (orchestrator's live test) | 690.91 | 335.21 | 2.06 | width | 1.164 | 666px |

**A correction to the brief, on the record.** The planning guidance called the short axis "the
main prize" and the end pad "secondary". At a 1280x820 window that is backwards: the drawing is
width-bound in every row of that table, so `scale` never touches `horizH`, and **the entire +9%
comes from the long axis**. The orchestrator's own live test confirms it — 795.58/690.91 = 1.151,
and their measured 578 -> 666 is 1.152.

The short-axis fix is still correct and still worth doing, for two reasons that are not this
window: it becomes the binding constraint whenever the container is wider than the viewBox aspect
(a large-monitor or short panel — at, say, 1800 x 700 it would be worth about +40%), and it stops
the content sitting off-centre inside a frame that is 87 units slack above it and 59 below.

The remaining gap to the 666px content-tight figure is the clipping guard: the ceiling assumes a
chip at its maximum pinned size, which at this window is about 60% larger than the chip actually
drawn. That is a deliberate trade — a clipped chip is a worse outcome than a slightly smaller
board — and it is the honest cost of not reading a live measurement.
