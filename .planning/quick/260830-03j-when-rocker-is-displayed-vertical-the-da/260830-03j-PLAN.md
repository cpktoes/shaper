<objective>
Make the ROCKER screen's station read-outs hold a constant on-screen size when the board is turned
nose-up, the way the TEMPLATE screen's callouts already do — so a shaper reading the vertical rocker
view gets the same size numbers they get on the template, instead of the tiny clustered figures in
the founder's screenshot.

**What the founder sees.** Rotated nose-up on a 9'4" board, the station cards and plain readings
render tiny around a thin board, with wide empty margins left and right. Nose-left looks right.

**Why.** The rocker viewer draws its cards and read-outs in fixed SVG user units
(`STATION_CARD_WIDTH` 74, `STATION_NAME_SIZE` 10, `STATION_VALUE_SIZE` 13), so their on-screen size
is whatever the drawing's fit gives them. Nose-left the frame is 900 units wide against a ~250-unit
cross axis, so a landscape panel fits it on WIDTH at roughly 1.1 px per unit. Nose-up the frame is
~300 units across and ~880 units along the board, so the same panel fits it on HEIGHT at roughly
0.70 px per unit. Everything shrinks by a third, cards included: a 13-unit value row that reads
14.4px nose-left reads 9.1px nose-up.

**What "match consistency with template" means here.** `components/viewer/callout-primitives.tsx`
already solved exactly this for the TEMPLATE screen: `CALLOUT_PX` names the target sizes in CSS
pixels, `pinnedCalloutSizes(fitScale)` converts them into the user units that render at those pixel
sizes by countering the live fit, and only the template EDITOR opts in (`pinCalloutText` on
`outline-viewer.tsx:485`) so the print path keeps drawing-scaled callouts. This plan gives the
rocker editor the same treatment, expressed in the shape the rocker drawing actually needs.

**The one design decision, and why it is one number.** A rocker card is not a template chip: its
width is DERIVED from the station pitch (`STATION_CARD_WIDTH = 12 * FIXED_SCALE - CARD_GUTTER`), so
its box and its type stack cannot be sized independently without the text escaping the card frame or
the cards colliding. So the pin is a single scalar — `cardScale` — applied to the card box, the two
rail anchors, the station-axis centring offset AND the whole type stack together. One number cannot
tear a card apart.

**Where the ceiling comes from, and why horizontal cannot move.** A card may grow only until
neighbouring stations' cards would touch along the station axis. The narrowest pitch any board
produces is the 12in tip-to-@12" span at the fixed scale, `12 * FIXED_SCALE` = 82 units, and the
card must fit inside that less one `CARD_GUTTER`:

- Nose-left a card presents its WIDTH along the station axis, and `STATION_CARD_WIDTH` is DEFINED
  as `12 * FIXED_SCALE - CARD_GUTTER`. The ceiling is therefore exactly **1.0** — horizontal is
  already saturated and provably cannot change. This is not a guard anyone has to remember; it
  falls out of the existing derivation.
- Nose-up a rotated card presents its HEIGHT along the station axis, `STATION_CARD_HEIGHT` = 35, so
  the ceiling is `74 / 35` ≈ **2.11** — the vertical rail has been carrying more than twice the
  headroom it uses.

At a typical editor panel the vertical fit is ~0.674 px/unit, so holding a 13-unit value row on
`CALLOUT_PX.value` (14px) needs ~1.60x — comfortably under the 2.11 ceiling, and the pin keeps
holding down to panels about 470px tall before it clamps and the callouts start shrinking with the
drawing again (the same graceful failure `MIN_PINNED_FIT_SCALE` gives the template).

**Why the frame reserves at the ceiling and not at the live scale.** The band depths feed the frame,
the frame feeds `useSvgFitScale`, and the fit scale is what produces the live card scale. Sizing the
band from the live scale would close that loop into a resize feedback loop — the defect
`outline-viewer.tsx` records as threat T-VOT-04 and answers with a module-constant
`MAX_CALLOUT_SIZES`. The rocker frame does the same: every frame extent is reserved at the ceiling,
so the frame is IDENTICAL for every card scale, and the live scale only moves the rail anchors
inside it. Horizontal reserves at ceiling 1.0, so its frame is untouched.

**The cost, stated plainly.** Nose-up, reserving the wider band grows the frame's long axis from
~881 to ~920 units, so the board itself draws about 4% smaller on screen while the callouts grow
about 55%. The cross-axis growth (300 -> 465 units) is free: that drawing is height-bound, so it
simply eats into the empty left/right margin the founder's screenshot shows.

**Deliberately out of scope.** The board-length label ("9'4"") is a display-face heading, not a
station read-out, and is left at its current size — scaling it too would push the long-axis reserve
far enough to cost the board a further ~4%. If the founder wants it larger, that is a follow-up.

Purpose: the rocker numbers are numbers a shaper cuts foam to. A figure they have to squint at is a
figure they will not trust.
Output: one scalar pin in the pure layout module, wired through the viewer, with the horizontal and
print paths pinned byte-identical by test.
</objective>
