# Every slider speaks its name and its printed value

## Goal

A screen reader at any slider in the app hears the slider's name and its value the way the row prints it — `Center
Thickness, 2 1/2"`, or `6.4 cm` for a Metric shaper — instead of `slider` and the bare number the slider runs on. Nothing
changes to the eye.

## Tasks

1. One rule in the shared row rather than a prop on forty call sites: `SliderRow` reads the spoken name and value back
   out of what it already prints — the label beside `displayValue` or `valueBox`, or the name and value folded into one
   label after the house separator (` — `). A row may still pass `sliderLabel` / `sliderValueText` to say something
   else (the Thinning Starts twins, the hand-set grid, the rail nudges). Pure and tested: `components/design/slider-spoken.ts`.
2. The six hand-rolled sliders the row never held get their own words: Board Length on TEMPLATE, FINS and VOLUME (the
   length as the row above prints it), FINS' Tail Width @ 12", RAILS' Family and Ratio.
3. The server-render pin in `slider-row.test.ts` updated deliberately: a row without the props now speaks its label
   line; a label-less row still says nothing.
4. Prove it in the browser: `e2e/slider-spoken-names.spec.ts` walks every design screen (ROCKER with a blank picked and
   its Advanced fold open, VOLUME and FINS with Import unticked, every closed Advanced fold opened) and holds every
   slider on the page to a name and a value that say at least what its row prints — in Imperial and in Metric, on a
   computer and both phones.
