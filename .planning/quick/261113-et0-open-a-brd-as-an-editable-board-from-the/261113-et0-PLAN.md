**What a file's bottom line does in the last inch.** Lift = the bottom's lowest at a station minus its lowest at the

(2 x value at 1" in, minus value at 2" in).

- **The Egg's nose is the misleading case.** Its bottom curve ends with a vertical tangent: in the last 1/4" the
  bottom rises 0.43" (3.978 -> 4.405) to meet the deck, and the nose ends 0.03" thick — a point. That last bit is the
  rounded tip, not the bottom; a blank matched to 4 3/8" would carry nearly half an inch too much nose rocker. The bottom
  carried straight on reaches 3 15/16". The Egg's tail is also a point (0.009" thick), but there the deck rolls down and
  the bottom runs straight, so the two readings agree (2.730 vs 2.719).
- **The Scoop's nose is real.** Its bottom steepens steadily through the last inch (0.33" per inch at 2"–1", 0.74" per
  inch in the last 1/4") and ends under a 0.29"-thick square nose: the bottom of the board really is at 4 1/8" at the
  tip. That is a scoop, and a shaper matching a blank wants it — our fit (3 5/8", fitted from 1" in) misses about 1/2".
- The 6'2 and the Fryer: true tip and straight-on agree within 0.06"; their tips end square (0.24"–0.57" thick).
- The app's own exports keep the bottom honest at the tips (within 0.03") and round only the deck, so a tip of ours
  that is not a point reads its very end (e.g. mid.brd's tail reads 1/2" thick where the board had about 3/4": the
  export rounded it — the file's truth, recorded as an honest limit).

**The rule (E3, also used for thickness):** a tip whose very end is thinner than 1/16" **comes to a point**. At a
point the file's lift and thickness at that tip are the bottom and the deck each carried straight on from 2" and 1" in
(lift = 2 x lift(1" in) - lift(2" in); thickness = max(0, 2 x thickness(1" in) - thickness(2" in))), and one line under
the table gives the point's own figures. Every other tip is read at its very end (the true tip). The centre and the two
12" stations are always read straight off the file. Nothing is clamped (they are the file's figures), except thickness
never below 0. The fit's own rule (follow the file from 1" in) is unchanged.

## How the app prints today, and how this sheet gets onto paper

The app has two print routes:

1. **CSS + `window.print()`** — the Summary order form (`app/design/summary/order-form.css` + `components/summary/use-print-fit.ts`)
   and RAILS' View Full Sized dialog (`app/design/rails/actual-size.css`): route-scoped print stylesheets that hide the
   rest of the screen. Getting these right on the founder's iPhone took several rounds (iOS ignores `@page` margins and
   absolute inch widths — the memory file records the saga).
2. **jsPDF + `components/template/print-pdf.ts`** — the Overview Sheet, Full Sized Template and Paper Saver (the founder,
   2026-10-15: "print the output rather than force a download"). A PDF is built in the browser; on a mouse it loads into
   an invisible 1-dot frame on the page and that frame prints itself; on an iPhone/iPad it opens in a tab opened inside
   the tap (Share -> Print); on Android it downloads. The page behind is never printed.

**This sheet uses route 2.** It prints only the sheet by construction (nothing on the home page or the dialog is
printed, so nothing has to be hidden), it is the route every dialog printout already takes and is proven on the
founder's devices, and it needs no print stylesheet on the home page. The page is **210 x 279.4 mm** — computed as the
smaller of `PAPER_MM.letter` and `PAPER_MM.a4` on each axis (`lib/geometry/template.ts`, millimetres, no inch figure):
A4's width by Letter's height, so it fits on either paper at 100% with no paper picker (the same "smaller of the two on
each axis" principle `use-print-fit.ts` uses). Black ink on white whatever the theme; nose on the left. CLAUDE.md Rule 2's
two paper-figure homes (`use-print-fit.ts`, `order-form.css`) are not touched and no third is added: this file names no
inch figure and no 25.4. jsPDF encodes `—`, `·`, `×` and `°` correctly in its helvetica (measured: bytes 97, b7, d7, b0).
