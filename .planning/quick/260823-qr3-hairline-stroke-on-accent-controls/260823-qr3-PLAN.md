# Quick Task 260823-qr3 — a stroke around the accent controls

The accent-filled controls are flat blobs of `#006994` on white: a solid slider thumb whose border is
the same colour as its fill, a filled range in an unbordered track, and filled toggle buttons. They
have no edge. Everything else in this app is drawn with a hairline — the outline viewers, the rail
plots, every box on the order form — so the controls are the one place the drafting language drops.

Giving them a `surf-black` hairline makes them read as drawn objects rather than painted areas, and
costs nothing in contrast: the accent fill stays exactly as it is.

## What gets a stroke

| Element | Now | After |
|---|---|---|
| Slider thumb | accent fill, accent border (invisible edge) | accent fill, **surf-black border** |
| Slider track | no border, accent range inside | **hairline border**, so the range sits in a drawn channel |
| Fin setup / model toggles (3 places) | `border-surf-accent-blue bg-surf-accent-blue` | `border-surf-black`, fill unchanged |
| Summary Print button | same | same treatment |

## Centralising the slider treatment first

The slider styling is one identical three-utility string —
`[&_[data-slot=slider-range]]:bg-surf-accent-blue [&_[data-slot=slider-thumb]]:border-surf-accent-blue [&_[data-slot=slider-thumb]]:bg-surf-accent-blue`
— repeated **14 times** across the outline (2), rails (5), volume (4) and fins (3) control files.
Editing fourteen copies to make one visual change is how they drift apart, so the treatment moves to
a single app-owned `.slider-accent` class in `globals.css` and the call sites become one class name.

`components/ui/slider.tsx` is deliberately left alone — it is shadcn-generated and may be regenerated,
so the app's styling belongs outside it. Targeting the primitive's `data-slot` hooks from CSS is what
the call sites were already doing through arbitrary variants; this just says it once.

## The thing to check

The track is 4px tall (`h-1`). A 1px border either side leaves 2px of interior for the accent range,
which may read as cramped or muddy. If it does, the track grows a little rather than the stroke being
dropped — verified by looking, not assumed.
