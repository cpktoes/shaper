Two judgment calls were open. Both are decided here. Do not reopen them mid-execution.

## Decision 1 — which sliders pair, and what happens to the four with no Angle partner

**The layout, top to bottom:**

```
Nose Rocker                                    full width
[ Nose Angle       |  Nose Smoothness  ]       paired
[ Nose Flatness    |  Tail Flatness    ]       paired
[ Tail Smoothness  |  Tail Angle       ]       paired
Tail Rocker                                    full width
```

Eight rows become five.

**Why the Angle/Smoothness pairs (locked by the orchestrator, restated for the executor):** in
`lib/geometry/rocker-drag.ts`, `solveSideProfileDrag` sets a tip's Angle and Smoothness *together*
from one dragged handle. They are one control with two numbers. Flatness is a different handle
entirely — that side's own centre control.

**Why the two Flatness sliders also pair.** This was the open question, and the answer is yes, pair
them, for three reasons that all point the same way:

1. *They are the centre pair.* Both control the flat spot through the middle of the board — how far
   the flat runs forward, and how far it runs aft. A shaper thinks about "the flat" as one thing
   with two ends, so one line reading `Nose Flatness | Tail Flatness` is closer to how the board is
   actually described than two separate lines.
2. *It costs nothing in reading order.* Nose Flatness and Tail Flatness are already adjacent in the
   list (positions 4 and 5). Folding them is a fold, not a move.
3. *It is what keeps the group symmetric.* The existing order is a mirror about its own centre, and
   the centre falls exactly between the two Flatness sliders. Fold the pairs and the mirror survives
   intact: nose lift / nose shape / the flat / tail shape / tail lift. Leave Flatness unpaired and
   the middle of the group is two lonely full-width rows in an otherwise two-up block — the fold
   looks half-done precisely where the symmetry should be strongest.

The stated worry — that this puts two *different* sides on one line, which the Angle/Smoothness
pairing does not — is real but does not survive contact with the domain. The Flatness row is not
"nose stuff next to unrelated tail stuff"; it is the one control in the group that inherently spans
the centre, and it sits on the centre line of the sidebar. Both cells keep their side prefix, so
nothing is ambiguous.

**Why Nose Rocker and Tail Rocker stay full-width:**

1. Pairing them would require lifting Tail Rocker from the bottom of the list to the top — the one
   reordering that breaks the mirrored nose-group-then-tail-group reading order we were told to
   preserve. Every other pair is a fold; that one would be a move.
2. They earn their own lines. Nose and tail rocker are the two headline numbers a shaper quotes
   about a board ("six and a quarter nose, two and a half tail"). As full-width bookends they frame
   the shape controls between them — the two figures you set first and check last.
3. Their labels carry inch fractions, which run longer than a percentage, so they use the width.

## Decision 2 — do NOT extract a shared `SliderRow`; duplicate the two wrapper classes instead

The minimal change wins here. Four reasons:

1. **This file carries a recorded decision against exactly that extraction.** Its header comment
   says each slider is written out individually "rather than factoring a wrapper that would hide
   each slider's own commit-callback call site." The reason still holds in this file specifically:
   every rocker slider converts differently on commit — inches to millimetres for the two lifts,
   a branded degrees value for the two angles, a plain percentage for the other four. The template's
   `SliderRow` flattens all of that behind one numeric callback prop. Overturning a documented,
   still-valid decision is not something a "put two sliders on one line" task gets to do on the way
   past.
2. **Extraction would touch the TEMPLATE screen, which nobody asked about.** `outline-controls.tsx`
   is the busiest sidebar in the app and its `SliderRow` carries disabled/hint/note behaviour and a
   clamped-depth warning. Rewiring it buys a re-verification burden on a screen with zero visible
   benefit to show for it.
3. **Two of five is a half-migration, which is worse than honest duplication.** `rail-controls.tsx`,
   `fin-controls.tsx` and `volume-controls.tsx` all hand-roll this same markup too. Extracting for
   two files leaves three stragglers and a component that is "the shared one" only sometimes.
4. **The `callout-primitives.tsx` precedent does not reach this far.** That extraction was worth it
   because the shared module carried real logic — rail and gutter constants, leader geometry, chip
   frames. A wrapper that adds one flex class around a label and a track carries no logic at all.

The extraction is still the right end state; it is just its own task. Task 2 records it as a pending
todo so it is deferred openly rather than quietly dropped. The trigger to do it: the moment a third
sidebar wants paired rows, extract once and migrate all five control files together.

## Decision 3 — the THICKNESS group is out of scope

Its five sliders are a nose-to-tail progression of one measurement (Nose Tip, Nose @ 12", Center,
Tail @ 12", Tail Tip), not pairs of controls that move together. Five is odd, so any fold leaves an
orphan, and pairing e.g. Nose @ 12" with Center would put two unrelated stations on a line. Left
exactly as it is, and Task 1 proves it byte-identical.
