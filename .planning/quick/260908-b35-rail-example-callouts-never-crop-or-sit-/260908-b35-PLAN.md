<design_decision>

## Why the plot gets extra width rather than the labels getting moved inboard

The four names on the apex column (Apex, Domed Taper, Rail Mk1, Tuck 1) are anchored at x = 0 — the
rail's apex, which is the drawing's rightmost structure by construction — and read rightward from
their own point, which is correct: they name marks that live on that column, and turning them
around to read leftward would run them back through the rail's own bands. The plot's box, though,
reserves only 0.15 inch (8.4 units) to the right of the apex, because that box was sized before
callouts existed. An SVG's own `overflow: hidden` then clips whatever sticks out. So the fault is
the box being too small for what it now draws, not the labels being in the wrong place.

The prototype never hit this because its interactive plot drew callouts as absolutely-positioned
HTML spans layered over the SVG, free to overflow their container, and its scroll-centring code
(Rails.dc.html line 1141) explicitly budgeted 120px of label extent to the right of the apex
column. This port draws them as real SVG `<text>`, which is the better decision — it is what makes
them print — so the box has to carry the budget the prototype kept outside it.

The pad is 96 viewBox units, taken from the shaper's own measurement rather than a guess: on the
INSTRUCTIONS card the widest right-hand name ended 79 units past the box's 486.8-unit right edge,
so 96 clears the worst measured case with about 17 units to spare.

## Why the room is opt-in instead of always added

Three other surfaces render this same component with no callouts at all — the VIEWER tab's stacked
section plots, the order form's first-sheet rail column, and the View Full Sized dialog — and plan
08-01 made their pixel identity an explicit contract. The full-sized dialog is the sharper case: it
converts the returned `width` back into real inches through `SCALE` to print the rail at its true
physical size, so a permanently padded width would print a rail at the wrong size, which is exactly
the kind of number this app exists to be trusted on. Adding the room only when names are actually
being drawn keeps all four surfaces exactly as they are, and the test pins the difference at
exactly the pad and nothing else.

## Why the axis rule and the line lift are two separate changes

The axis rule is a parity fix: the prototype's de-overlap pass has one more step than the port
kept — a `maxAllowedY` ceiling, and a whole-cluster shift up whenever a stacked cluster's lowest
label would fall past it. Nothing about it is new design, and it is restored verbatim.

The line lift is genuinely new, and small: four of the ten names are anchored exactly ON a drawn
line (Deck 3 and Deck 1 on the top deck line, Bottom Tuck 1 and Bottom Tuck 3 on the x-axis) and
are drawn with `dominantBaseline="middle"`, so the text straddles both the line and its own dot.
Every other name is anchored somewhere in open space and reads fine, which is why the lift is a
per-anchor value in the existing anchor table rather than a blanket offset on all ten.

They are separate commits because they are separately reviewable, and because the lift is applied
before the ceiling: the bottom-edge names are lifted 8 units first, and the ceiling then has the
final say on where the whole cluster sits.

## Why the ticks move with the names

The plot draws a small `DimensionTick` slash at each callout's position and the name beside it. The
lift moves both together. That is deliberate and no leader line is added: the tick marks where the
name is, and the mark's real position is already drawn — it is the coloured dot the plot puts at
every segment endpoint, which stays exactly where it was.
