# Orchestrator brief for quick 261015-my3 — the WINGS close-up, as large as it can be

The founder looked at the wing fixes (quick 261015-hz7, merged on main, not pushed) on the Mid-length preset with
a wing 1 at 20 1/2" and a wing 2 at 5 15/16", both 3/8" deep with a 1/2" run and a 15/16" corner radius, on a
~1900 px-wide browser. Their screenshot is beside this brief (`261015-my3-founder-screenshot.webp`). Their words:

> "The thickness of the lines need to match the template viewer. The labels of the right side dims don't make
> sense (ahead of wing 2... it is wing 2); however, it doesn't really matter since I don't think we need any dims
> on the right. Lets put all the wing data in the data cards on the left (add a Run and Radius value to each card
> and an offset from fin when locked to the fin too). Tail block can be written directly under the tail itself,
> and the Tail Width can be on the right side also. This should allow the wing view to be as large as possible."

## What changes (the close-up on the WINGS tab only; the VIEWER, prints and every other screen are untouched)

1. **Line weights match the VIEWER on screen.** Verified cause: `components/outline/wing-close-up.tsx` draws the
   board's ink line at a raw `strokeWidth={2}` (line ~380), the no-wings dashed curve at `1.5` (~370) and the fin
   marks at `2` (~474), in the close-up's own viewBox units — but the close-up's fit (`useSvgFitScale`, about 0.88
   at 1280 × 800) differs from the VIEWER's, so the same "2" lands at a different width on screen. The VIEWER
   (`outline-viewer.tsx`) draws its outline at `2 * zoomUnit` and its reference lines at `zoomUnit` in ITS units,
   i.e. 2 × its own fit in px. Requirement: at a given window, the close-up's board line, fin marks, reference
   lines (stringer, 12" station, widepoint), the dashed no-wings curve and its leaders/extension lines all render
   at the same on-screen px width as the corresponding VIEWER line at that window. Decide how (pin to screen px via
   `handleUnit` × the VIEWER's own px weight, computing the VIEWER's fit for the same panel box from its frame
   maths, or another way) — measure both at 1280 × 800, 1024 × 768 and the phone boxes and put the numbers in the
   plan. Grab-point rings are already pinned to screen px; leave them.
2. **Nothing on the right** — the founder, after the first brief: "the tail @ 12" data can also be on the left.
   Nothing on the right." Remove the right rail entirely: the "Ahead of wing n" / "Behind wing n" read-outs, the
   depth dimension across each step, the run dimension along it, and the `Tail @ 12"` read-out's rail position.
   The tail's width at 12" (Metric `Tail @ 30.5 cm`) moves to the LEFT gutter as a card in the same dress as the
   wing cards (name `TAIL @ 12"`, its width as the value), with a horizontal leader to the board's left edge at the
   12" station, spread with the wing cards by the same rules (inside the drawing, the pair's visible gap). This
   deliberately departs from the drawing grammar's "outputs on the right rail" for THIS view only (the founder's
   ruling); the VIEWER keeps its right rail. The frame then needs only the board's own side room on the right —
   decide whether the board sits centred in what is left or hugs the gutter, whichever draws it larger.
3. **Tail block written directly under the tail.** The tail block's width (`4"` / its Metric dim) sits centred
   under the tail block line, below the board, with its name (`Tail block`) — no rail, no leader. A pin or round
   tail (no block) shows nothing there. Keep the drafting grammar: text never crosses the board (sketch 004).
4. **Every wing number in that wing's card on the left.** Each WING card (today a 2-line chip: `WING 1 · FIN` /
   `3/8" @ 20 1/2"`) becomes a data card with rows: the position and depth line as today (`3/8" @ 20 1/2"`), `Run`
   and `Radius` (the radius actually drawn — the effective one, never more than fits), and — only when the wing is
   locked — `Offset` from its fin's trailing edge (`+1/4"`, `0"`, `−1/2"`; front fins for wing 1, rear fins for
   wing 2 on a quad). Dims and marks per Rule 2: position is a dim (Metric cm, one decimal); depth, run, radius and
   offset are marks (whole mm). Decide the card's layout (the chip frame grown to more rows, a two-column row for
   Run · Radius, etc.) within the drafting grammar (`components/viewer/callout-primitives.tsx`: `CalloutChipFrame`,
   the pinned sizes, the hz7 value-fit rule — every text inside its card with ≥ 4 px each side, worst cases:
   Imperial `13/16" @ 47 13/16"`, Metric `25 mm @ 152.4 cm`, `Radius 2 15/16"`, `Offset −2 15/16"`). Cards keep
   their leader to the wing's apex and the hz7 spread rules (inside the drawing top and bottom, the pair's visible
   gap between cards).
5. **The position dimensions on the left go too** — CONFIRMED by the founder (2026-10-15, with a screenshot of the
   20 1/2" and 5 15/16" vertical dims): "we also don't need the off tail callouts since that info is now in the
   cards."  the vertical station dims beside the cards
   (`frame.stationDimX`, `data-wing-station-dim`) duplicate the card's `@ 20 1/2"` and cost gutter width.
6. **As large as possible.** With nothing on the right and the left gutter holding only the cards,
   shrink `railRight`, `gutterLeft` and any slack in `components/outline/wing-view-layout.ts` (CLOSE_UP and
   CLOSE_UP_COMPACT) to what the remaining text needs at the pinned callout floor, so the board draws larger.
   Re-derive the compact switch (`useCompactFrame`) and the window rules unchanged (26" tail window, grows past 24"
   with 3" of room). Measure and record the fit / px per inch before and after on the five boards at 1280 × 800,
   1024 × 768 and the four phone boxes; nothing may leave the drawing.

7. **Zoom, like the VIEWER** — the founder (2026-10-15): "wings tab also needs zoom capabilities like the
   viewer." Today the close-up has none (`wings-panel.tsx` ~22 and `wing-close-up.tsx` ~62 say so). Give it the
   same continuous zoom the TEMPLATE viewer got in quick 261007-fnz, through the shared
   `components/viewer/zoom-viewport.tsx`: `ViewerZoomProvider` around the WINGS tab's content (its own provider, so
   switching tabs starts each at 1x, as ROCKER's tabs do), `useViewerZoom(svgRef, baseViewBox, { touch: "viewer" })`
   on the close-up's svg exactly as `outline-viewer.tsx` ~781–792 does (the drawing has grab points, so one finger
   stays on a grab point and two fingers pinch/pan — the TEMPLATE viewer's own rule), and `ViewerZoomControl` in the
   WINGS toolbar's row under the icons as `outline-editor.tsx` ~254 places it. Every raw size the close-up draws
   (the matched line weights from item 1, dashes via `zoomDashToken(token, zoomUnit)` — today hard-coded to `1` at
   ~391/401/412 — the cards, leaders, the tail-block words, the grab points, their hit radius) keeps its SCREEN size
   at any zoom (`zoomUnit` / `handleUnit` × the zoom, the fnz rule). The grab-point drag must still map the pointer
   correctly when zoomed and panned (today `toBoardPoint` reads the root svg's matrix and notes "never zoomed" at
   ~177 — read the zoomed content's own matrix as the VIEWER's `toBoardPoint` does), and the window still holds
   still while a point is held. The window's growth past 24" sets the BASE viewBox; zoom works within it. Nothing
   about the zoom is saved. Tests: extend `e2e/viewer-zoom.spec.ts` (or `e2e/wings.spec.ts`) with the WINGS tab —
   the wheel zooms toward the pointer, the buttons and double-click reset, a drag pans above 1x, line and word
   screen sizes hold at 6x, a grab point drags correctly at 3x, and a phone pinch on the Android project.
