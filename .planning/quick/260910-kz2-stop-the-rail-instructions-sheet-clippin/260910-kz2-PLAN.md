<objective>
Stop the printed Rail Band Instructions sheet from silently throwing away its example rail drawing
on a narrow page.

Purpose: the sheet's job is to name every mark the rest of the order form talks about, and the
example rail drawing is what does the naming. On a small page it currently gets squeezed to nothing
and the sheet quietly clips the rest — no second page, no warning, no drawing. A shaper printing to
anything smaller than about half a letter sheet gets a reference page that references nothing.

Output: the figure learns to fit the box it is given rather than only the width, and the sheet
divides its height between its two drawings so that when the paper runs short they shrink together
instead of one of them disappearing. Every page the shaper prints today is byte-for-byte unchanged.
</objective>

<the_shape_of_the_change>
Two files. About sixty lines. No new dependency, no route, no database, no stylesheet edit.

**1. `components/rails/rail-plan-side-figure.tsx` — the figure learns a second way to fit.**

A new optional prop `fit`, taking `"width"` (the default, today's markup exactly) or `"height"`.
The name and its two values deliberately mirror `components/rails/rail-section-plot.tsx:127`, which
already carries the same prop for the same reason — and which the example rail sitting directly
above this figure already uses at `fit="height"`. One vocabulary for one idea.

In `"height"` mode, three things change and nothing else does:

- The card fills the box it is handed and lays its contents out as a column, so the row inside can
  take the height rather than the row's contents dictating it.
- The row becomes the flexible child of that column, allowed to shrink to nothing.
- The drawing box takes its HEIGHT from the row and derives its WIDTH from the aspect ratio it
  already declares, instead of taking its width from a fixed basis and deriving its height. Its
  `aspectRatio`, its four inner columns, the plan `<img>`, both SVGs, the station labels, the taper
  note and the key are all untouched.

Two exported constants come out of this so the sheet does not have to know the figure's internals:
`FIGURE_CARD_CHROME_PX` (the card's own padding and border) and `FIGURE_MAX_CARD_HEIGHT_PX`, which
is `FIGURE_MAX_RENDERED_HEIGHT` plus that chrome — 502, which is what the browser measures as
501.98. Both are derived from constants already in the file; neither is typed as a bare number.

**2. `components/summary/rail-instructions-sheet.tsx` — the sheet divides its height.**

The heading stays where it is and stops being able to shrink. The two drawings move into their own
column below it, and that column is what the leftover height belongs to. Inside it:

- the example rail keeps exactly the band it has today — flexible, centred, `min-h-0`;
- the figure sits in a box of `min(FIGURE_MAX_CARD_HEIGHT_PX px, FIGURE_MAX_BODY_SHARE_PERCENT%)`
  and is asked for at `fit="height"`.

`FIGURE_MAX_BODY_SHARE_PERCENT` is 85, named and commented in this file because it is a decision
about how this sheet divides its paper, not about how the figure draws itself. Above about 536 dots
the `min()` picks the pixel term and the sheet is exactly today's sheet; below it the percentage
takes over and the two drawings shrink together.

The wrapper column is load-bearing, not tidiness: a percentage height resolves against the flex
container, so without it the 85% would be 85% of the whole body including the heading, and the
example rail would go negative below about 421 dots.
</the_shape_of_the_change>
