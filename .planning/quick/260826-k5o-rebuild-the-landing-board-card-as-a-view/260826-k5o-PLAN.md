<decisions_this_plan_settles>
Three questions were left open for this plan. Settled here, and implemented as written —
do not hedge by building alternatives.

**1. The Continue card gets no window.**
It loses the outer line and takes the same 12px frame as the preset cards, so the two
cards' frames stay identical. It does NOT get a white box around its text. Two reasons: a
viewing window frames a drawing, and this card has none — a white box with nothing in it is
a window onto nothing; and the preset cards' own name/descriptor/CTA sit directly on the
sand, so boxing the identical three lines of text on the Continue card would put the same
content on two different surfaces across one grid. Parity is kept where it is actually
measurable: identical outer className, same fill, frame width, radius, type and hover/focus
behaviour. The Continue card being shorter than a preset card is pre-existing (it has never
had a thumbnail) and is not changed by this task.

**2. Padding and radius: 12 and 12, radius `rounded-lg` (10px).**
The outer card's inset goes from 16px to 12px, and the new window box takes a 12px inset.
That reproduces exactly the geometry measured on `/design/rails` — 12px canvas frame, 12px
panel inset — so the card is the design screen's rhythm at card scale rather than a
near-miss. It also matters for board size: adding a 12px window inset while leaving the
outer at 16px would cost the board drawing about 12% of its width; going to 12/12 costs
about 8% and buys back the difference. Intended visual result: an even sand band of one
width around three sides of the window, with the text sitting on that same sand below it.
The card's own `rounded-xl` (14px) is untouched — corner parity with the panel was never
asked for. The window box takes `rounded-lg` (10px), matching both the thumbnail well
inside it and the tabbed panel's inner content card.

**3. The window box does NOT get `overflow-hidden`.**
The thumbnail well sits 12px inside the window's content box, so its corners never reach the
window's rounded corners — there is nothing to clip and no double-rounding. Adding a second
clipping context here could only ever crop the board silently if the well later grew. Task 3
measures `overflow: visible` on the box and confirms the board's drawn box is unchanged.
</decisions_this_plan_settles>
