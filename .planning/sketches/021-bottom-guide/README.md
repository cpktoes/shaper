# Sketch 021: The BOTTOM page's GUIDE tab, with the rulings built in

## Decision so far (the founder, 2026-11-07, second round)
*"The Guide looks awesome. Love everything about it."* And: *"I don't think we need the 3 readouts. I think the space is
better used for visualization."* So the three figures under the drawing are gone and the drawings take the room; the
litres and the stringer are one line at the top of DATA. He could not find the ruler (*"I don't see a ruler of the ticks
you talk of"*) — it draws only on a wide screen, and only for the cut being edited; `pictures/desktop-ruler-close.png`
shows it. The starting numbers go to research (*"Do a deeper research to see what is most common for each and report
back. The one that stands out is the tear drop concave seems a bit short."*) — the findings and the numbers proposed from
them are in the todo `2026-11-06-set-the-bottom-contours-starting-numbers-from-the-shop.md`, for his correction.

## Third round (the founder, 2026-11-08)
*"This all seems really great."* On the page's place — *"Is between Rocker and Rails the right place to put this? When I
shape this is where I'd add it, but the fin placement is likely to have an affect too."* — the answer stands: BOTTOM stays
between ROCKER and RAILS (RAILS works from what the bottom leaves), and the fins are read, not waited for: the app
calculates the front-fin station and toe from the outline, which is why the sketch already ticks FRONT FINS and opens every
tail cut deepest there. *"Let's leave as is"* — so three notes for the phase rather than the sketch: store the fin-driven
stations (a tail cut's Deepest At, the channels' start and deepest) as a distance from the front fins so a fin move on FINS
carries them; draw the fin boxes on the BOTTOM top view, not just the station line; and show the contour under the fins on
the FINS tail drawing. Then: *"I think every page needs a guide like this. Super useful on what each parameter does to/for
the board. Would also be nice to add a 'how to shape' note too."* — every card now carries a **How to shape** note in the
shop's words (first drafts), and the guide on every page is the todo `2026-11-08-a-guide-tab-on-every-page.md`.

## Fourth round (the founder, 2026-11-08): the numbers are in
*"Put the proposed starting numbers into the sketch, and I think we're done here. Maybe don't show the finished rails from
rails by default."* So every name now opens on the numbers the research proposed (the table in the starting-numbers todo):
the teardrop 1/4" deep and 36" long, 2 1/4" in from the rails; the bellies 3/8", deepest at the wide point; the deep single
flattened in the last 4"; the shallow single from a third back, deepest at the front fins; the tail double 1" off the rail
from a foot ahead of the fins; the panel and rolled vees from a third of the length up, peaking at the fins; the tri-plane's
flat panel to 4" from the rail; the channels 5/16" deep from a foot ahead of the front fins, the outer pair longest. The
finished rail from RAILS is off until ticked. The sketch is done; the sentences, shop notes and numbers stay his to correct
as the phase is planned.

## Fifth round (the founder, 2026-11-08): the section fills the box
*"Can we maximize the width of the cross-section? I like that it doesn't scale according to station width, but perhaps we
can make the section near full screen width at the board's wide point."* The section keeps one scale for every station,
and that scale now puts the wide point's section across 95% of the box (20 dots of air each side) instead of 83%. The
straightedge overhangs the rails as far as the box allows. Near the wide point there is no longer room beside the rail
for the rail-lift value, so it reads under the straightedge at the right rail (*3/8" rail lift*), with its dimension lines
just outside each rail; at a narrow station it reads beside the rail as before, and the stringer-left dimension draws
only when there is room for it. A gauge label is drawn only when it would read at least 1/16" (1 mm), never *0"*.

## Design Question
The founder's rulings on sketch 020 (2026-11-07): *"Stagger looks good now. B: Inside Tail, Channel Default = none. Wings
rule should have a selector, narrowest outline by default. Run default is parallel to stringer."* And one more ask the same
breath: *"We also need a third tab that is informational and teaches the user VERY briefly (like 1-2 sentences) and with a
cartoon, what each contour type does, is, and looks like. With so many Vee options, I think we need a clear idea how they
differ."*

So this sketch is 020 with the rulings in and the variants gone, plus the tab:

- **B** — the channels live inside the TAIL card under the tail's own cut, "cut after the Flat Exit"; the phone's switch is
  NOSE · CENTER · TAIL again. A flat tail with channels says *"Nothing is cut in this section but the channels."*
- **None to start** — every board opens with no channels (the Shortboard keeps its deep single, the Fish its panel vee, the
  winged Fish its extending single). Picking 4, 6 or 8 gives that count's starting width (2 1/2" · 1 3/4" · 1 1/4") unless the
  shaper has already set a width of their own; a set opens 5/16" deep from a foot ahead of the front fins, the outer pair
  longest with a 2" stagger (fourth round).
- **The finished rail from RAILS is off** until its box is ticked (fourth round).
- **Parallel to the stringer** is the run a set opens with; the Run menu reads *Parallel · With the rail · Toe-in · 3.2°*,
  short enough to read whole beside Longest.
- **The wing rule is a selector** — a WINGS row above the three sections, shown only on a board with a wing (*1/4" wing at
  11 1/2"*): *Measure Off Rail from* — **Narrowest outline** (opening) or *Natural outline, before the wing* — with one line
  under it saying what each means. The sketch bar's switch is gone.
- **No readouts in the viewer** (second round): the drawings take the room; *Foam off 0.72 L · 28.89 L now, 29.61 L
  before contours · the stringer finishes 2 1/16" at the center, planed 2 1/4"* is the first line of DATA.
- **GUIDE**, the third tab after VIEWER and DATA: nineteen cards — the six nose cuts, six center, six tail, and the channels —
  each with its name, a cartoon, two sentences — what it is and looks like (in ink), then what it does (muted) — and, under
  a dashed rule, a *How to shape* note: which lines to pencil, what to cut with, where to take the most, what to check with
  (third round). The cartoon
  is the board the shaper has open, drawn by the VIEWER's own code: from above with that one cut's shade and the pencil lines
  a shaper would rule, the dash-dot cutting line where the section is taken, and the section across it at its deepest point,
  Height ×8, with the planed bottom as a dashed straightedge. So the five vees draw five different sections — the Panel Vee's
  two straight planes meeting at a corner, the Rolled Vee's rounded ones with no corner, the Tri-Plane's flat panel between
  its planes, the Reverse Vee at the nose, the Spiral Vee's two troughs inside lifted rails — and the three single concaves
  under the nose differ in where their shade closes. The cuts on the board now are tagged *On this board*; switch boards and
  every cartoon redraws on the new outline. The sentences are first drafts for the founder's pen.

## What to Look For
- The GUIDE's sentences: right, wrong, or too long. The founder asked for *"VERY briefly (like 1-2 sentences)"*; every
  card carries two, and now a How to shape note of one or two more — the shop's words are his to correct.
- Whether the cartoon says enough — the top view and the section at ×8 — or whether the vees want a third drawing (three
  stacked sections, say, to show a spiral vee twisting along the tail).
- Whether a card should be tappable to put that cut on the board (not built; the tab is informational, as asked).
- The WINGS row's wording and place — above the sections, one setting for the whole bottom.
- Still open from 019 and 020: the ruler under the section (computer only, the live cut's marks only), the starting
  numbers now in the sketch (the researched set, his to correct), and whether a staggered set's deepest station or ends
  should stagger too.
